# THC Lesson — Seed Germination: Evidence Before Diagnosis
Status: DRAFT — scientific and media review required

Audience: adult plant-science learners
Topic ID: THC-SOC-EDU-GERM-001

## Learning objectives
Distinguish germination from emergence, identify the radicle and cotyledons, explain why water, oxygen and temperature interact, and separate direct observations from diagnoses.

## Lesson
A seed is a living embryo with stored reserves enclosed by protective tissues. Under suitable conditions, germination begins with water uptake (imbibition). Rehydration supports renewed metabolic activity; oxygen is needed for aerobic respiration. The embryonic root (radicle) emerges through the seed covering. In strict seed-physiology usage, visible radicle protrusion marks completion of germination. Emergence of the seedling above the substrate is a later event, not the same milestone.

The emerging root grows and may develop root hairs that increase the root's absorptive surface. Cotyledons are embryonic seed leaves; the first true leaves develop subsequently. In a photo sequence, label these stages accurately rather than showing a mature plant as evidence of seed germination.

Water, oxygen availability and temperature interact. A waterlogged medium can have low oxygen availability even though moisture is abundant. Temperature affects the rate of biochemical processes, but no single germination temperature is universally optimal for all species, seed lots or conditions. Seed viability, dormancy, seed age, storage history and contamination are additional confounders.

## Observation demonstration
Use the same seed lot and a recorded observation protocol. Record the observation date/time, number of seeds, substrate, moisture method, temperature measurement and whether radicle protrusion is visible. Photograph at a consistent scale and angle with a millimeter ruler. Calculate observed germination percentage only when the denominator and endpoint are defined; a sample of five seeds is a demonstration, not a reliable population estimate. Do not infer pathogen infection solely from a failed germination event.

## Common misconceptions
- 'No sprout above soil' does not necessarily mean the seed never germinated.
- Cotyledons are not the first true leaves.
- More water does not always mean better germination because oxygen can become limiting.
- A generic stock image of a mature plant cannot document a germination sequence.
