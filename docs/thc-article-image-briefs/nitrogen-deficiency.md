# THC Article #2 Image Brief — Nitrogen Deficiency

Article slug: `nitrogen-deficiency`

Visual standard: photorealistic cannabis plant-science photography or academic studio illustration; neutral white balance; no yellow cast; accurate leaf morphology; uncluttered backgrounds; educational rather than dispensary/stoner styling; mobile-readable crops; no text baked into photographs unless the asset is specifically an infographic.

## Core image set

1. **Hero — whole-plant nitrogen deficiency pattern**
   - Mature cannabis plant in controlled indoor environment.
   - Lower canopy visibly pale/yellow while upper/new growth remains greener.
   - Show enough of the entire plant to make canopy position obvious.
   - Role: `hero`
   - Suggested ID: `nitrogen-deficiency-hero`

2. **Early symptom close-up**
   - Older lower fan leaf showing mild general chlorosis beginning at the leaflet tips and spreading inward.
   - Avoid severe necrosis; this image should teach early detection.
   - Role: `diagnostic`
   - Suggested ID: `early-lower-leaf-chlorosis`

3. **Progression series**
   - Four matched fan leaves representing healthy, early, moderate, and severe nitrogen deficiency.
   - Same apparent genotype/leaf shape and neutral lighting.
   - Role: `comparison`
   - Suggested ID: `nitrogen-progression-series`

4. **Canopy-location comparison**
   - Whole cannabis plant with lower/older leaves showing chlorosis while young apex remains greener.
   - No arrows/text in the photo version; location should read visually.
   - Role: `diagnostic`
   - Suggested ID: `lower-canopy-pattern`

5. **Nitrogen vs magnesium comparison**
   - Two cannabis fan leaves side by side.
   - Nitrogen example: broader, more uniform chlorosis.
   - Magnesium example: stronger interveinal chlorosis with greener veins.
   - Academic comparison-board presentation.
   - Role: `comparison`
   - Suggested ID: `nitrogen-vs-magnesium`

6. **Nitrogen vs root-zone stress comparison**
   - Two whole plants or matched lower-canopy examples.
   - One demonstrates classic lower-leaf nitrogen pattern with otherwise normal posture.
   - One demonstrates yellowing plus persistent droop associated with saturated root-zone stress.
   - Role: `comparison`
   - Suggested ID: `nitrogen-vs-root-stress`

7. **Root-zone diagnostic visual**
   - Clean cutaway/studio view of a cannabis root ball in a container.
   - Visually distinguish healthy aerated roots/media from oversaturated low-oxygen conditions.
   - No grotesque disease exaggeration.
   - Role: `diagram`
   - Suggested ID: `root-zone-check`

8. **Normal senescence vs deficiency**
   - Two flowering cannabis plants at comparable stage.
   - Left: limited aging of oldest fan leaves late in flower with healthy upper canopy.
   - Right: broader rapidly progressing nitrogen-deficiency pattern.
   - Role: `comparison`
   - Suggested ID: `senescence-vs-deficiency`

9. **Excess nitrogen contrast**
   - Healthy/adequate plant beside high-nitrogen example with unusually dark green foliage and restricted/abnormal vigor.
   - Avoid presenting a single leaf color as definitive; this supports the article section on excess.
   - Role: `comparison`
   - Suggested ID: `deficiency-vs-excess-nitrogen`

10. **Measurement workflow photograph**
    - Grower-science desktop/root-zone scene with calibrated pH meter, EC meter, nutrient solution sample, plant label/log, and cannabis plant nearby.
    - Clean academic laboratory/grow-room aesthetic.
    - Role: `supporting`
    - Suggested ID: `measure-before-correcting`

## Infographic 1 — Nitrogen Deficiency Diagnostic Path

Title: **NITROGEN DEFICIENCY: READ THE PATTERN FIRST**

Layout:
- Central cannabis plant silhouette/photo with upper, middle, and lower canopy clearly separated.
- Step 1: Older/lower leaves first?
- Step 2: Broad chlorosis or interveinal/spotting pattern?
- Step 3: Growth slowing?
- Step 4: Check plant stage.
- Step 5: Check root-zone moisture, pH, and EC.
- Step 6: Review feed history.
- Step 7: Track progression before correcting.

Include a clear warning: **Yellow leaves alone do not prove nitrogen deficiency.**

Role: `infographic`
Suggested ID: `nitrogen-diagnostic-infographic`

## QA requirements

- Cannabis leaf morphology must be botanically plausible.
- Symptoms must match the educational claim made by the adjacent section.
- Avoid uniform yellow filters or warm color grading that interferes with diagnosis.
- Alt text must describe the visible diagnostic evidence, not merely repeat the file name.
- Any externally sourced image must have documented reuse rights and credit.
- Generated educational visuals must not imitate a specific published figure.
