# Approved THC Education Infographic Style

Status: CANONICAL APPROVED VISUAL STANDARD
Scope: THC / Teaching Healthy Cultivation education, learning center, encyclopedia, curriculum, Atlas, course support, printable infographics, and related visual information.
Effective date: 2026-09-09

This policy records the visual standard explicitly approved from the current reference set. It supersedes any older rule that treated cream/green academic layouts, boxed panels, or dark educational posters as inherently rejected.

## Core quality standard

Approved THC education visuals must look like finished educational publishing, not a simple generated poster. Every visual should combine strong information design with topic-specific scientific or cultivation imagery.

Required qualities:
- immediately clear title, subtitle, and reading order;
- high information density without crowding;
- clean grid, intentional spacing, consistent alignment, and balanced margins;
- readable body copy at intended display/print size;
- meaningful diagrams, arrows, tables, charts, cutaways, callouts, or comparisons where they improve understanding;
- realistic/high-detail cannabis photography or botanical/scientific illustration when imagery is used;
- subject-specific imagery rather than decorative filler;
- accurate terminology, labels, units, relationships, ranges, and scientific concepts;
- professional iconography used as secondary navigation/support, not as a substitute for the teaching visual;
- production-ready finish with no placeholders, malformed text, broken images, obvious AI artifacts, or inconsistent components;
- THC / Teaching Healthy Cultivation branding used consistently and with restraint;
- dtfseeds.com branding may be present where appropriate;
- source masters should be suitable for high-resolution/300-DPI print workflows when a printable master is expected.

## Approved visual families

### A. Dark premium / photo-rich educational infographic

Use for subjects such as harvest, post-harvest, storage, degradation, quality, curing, environmental risk, and other topics where premium photographic presentation improves comprehension.

Visual traits:
- black or charcoal base;
- warm cream/off-white headline text;
- olive/forest green educational accents;
- high-quality cannabis flower, equipment, environmental, microscopic, or process photography;
- restrained metallic/warm line work where appropriate;
- modular information panels with strong contrast;
- icons and arrows used to guide the reader through causal relationships;
- polished editorial/scientific-poster feel rather than a game/card feel.

### B. Light scientific / cream-green educational infographic

Use for botany, root-zone science, air, VPD, lighting, plant anatomy, nutrient cycling, species overview, and other instructional/reference topics.

Visual traits:
- cream, white, or very light neutral base;
- dark forest green as the primary THC education color;
- navy/blue, red, orange, violet, or other semantic accent colors when they communicate data or category meaning;
- realistic/high-detail botanical illustration, roots, leaves, plant structures, microbes, equipment, or scientific diagrams;
- strong section bars and clearly grouped modules;
- tables/charts may carry more of the page when the subject is quantitative;
- educational textbook / university-extension quality rather than simple poster art.

## Typography and hierarchy

- Prefer bold condensed or strong sans-serif display typography for major titles.
- Major headings must be visually dominant and readable from a reduced preview.
- Section headings should form a consistent second level.
- Body copy must remain readable without zooming excessively.
- Avoid tiny dense labels, decorative type, novelty fonts, or inconsistent capitalization that weakens comprehension.
- Do not allow generated text artifacts, misspellings, malformed symbols, incorrect subscripts/superscripts, or illegible microcopy.

## Imagery standard

Primary teaching imagery should be one or more of:
- realistic cannabis photography;
- high-detail botanical illustration;
- scientific cutaway/cross-section;
- accurate root-zone/soil/microbial illustration;
- equipment or environmental photography tied directly to the lesson;
- explanatory chart, matrix, pathway diagram, or process visualization.

Reject as primary teaching imagery:
- generic flat cannabis clip art;
- low-detail cartoon plant icons;
- unrelated stock imagery;
- decorative leaves used where a scientific structure/process should be shown;
- obviously repeated/generated filler panels;
- low-resolution, blurry, warped, or artifact-heavy imagery.

## Information-design standard

A finished infographic should teach through its layout. Depending on topic, use:
- numbered sequences;
- cause/effect pathways;
- side-by-side comparisons;
- risk or target ranges;
- stage-based guides;
- labeled plant anatomy;
- arrows showing movement/flow;
- tables or matrices for quantitative relationships;
- key takeaways and practical application notes.

Do not add complexity for decoration. Every panel should either explain, compare, warn, quantify, orient, or summarize.

## Scientific accuracy and claims

- Verify scientific values before publication.
- Use exact units and explain approximations.
- Avoid presenting working guidance as an absolute cultivar rule when biology/environment can shift the target.
- Distinguish correlation, mechanism, practical guidance, and established fact.
- Do not invent percentages, ranges, pathways, citations, or cultivar-specific outcomes to make a visual look complete.
- When a topic requires nuance, use a clear note rather than oversimplifying the science.

## Branding

Approved branding may include:
- THC;
- Teaching Healthy Cultivation;
- dtfseeds.com;
- restrained cannabis-leaf motifs;
- education/project badges where they do not compete with the lesson.

Branding should frame the educational content, not dominate it.

## Approval and publication rule

- User-approved visuals are protected from automated cleanup unless later explicitly rejected.
- New/generated visuals are DRAFT by default, even if they resemble the approved style.
- A filename, series number, palette, or folder location does not constitute approval.
- A visual must pass content accuracy, readability, rendering, and style review before becoming an approved public master.
- Supporting/reference images may remain in source storage while being excluded from public infographic galleries.
- Rejected assets must not be silently republished through renamed files, alternate extensions, derivative generation, WordPress sync, or fallback image logic.

## Current interpretation of the approved reference set

The approved references establish that both dark and light layouts are valid. Their common characteristics are professional publication quality, high-detail cannabis/scientific imagery, strong hierarchy, useful information density, accurate diagrams/data, and deliberate THC educational branding.

Therefore, do **not** reject an asset merely because it uses cream/green colors, boxed panels, a black background, or cannabis imagery. Reject it when its execution is simplistic, low-detail, inaccurate, unreadable, generic, broken, or materially below this standard.
