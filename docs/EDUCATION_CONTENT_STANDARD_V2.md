# THC Education Content Standard v2

## Purpose

THC education should outperform ordinary cultivation guides by combining beginner clarity, field usefulness, technical depth, evidence discipline, purposeful visuals, interactive tools, diagnostics, and structured learning in one connected system.

This standard does not measure quality by word count alone. A page is strong when a learner can orient quickly, gather the right evidence, make a defensible decision, verify the result, and move to deeper material without losing context.

## Competitive benchmark translated into requirements

Strong public cultivation resources commonly excel in one or two dimensions:
- simple beginner-first explanations and symptom-first discovery;
- broad stage/topic navigation and grow-guide coverage;
- concrete procedures, schedules, downloadable references, and operational detail;
- evidence-focused extension or academic guidance.

THC must combine those strengths while adding stronger evidence boundaries, measurement literacy, cross-linking, diagnostics, and progress-oriented instruction.

## Required learner layers

Every major subject page must provide all of the following.

1. **Quick answer**
   - A concise orientation that tells the learner what matters before they read the deep explanation.
   - It must not substitute a universal recipe for context-dependent plant science.

2. **Observe**
   - At least four concrete observations.
   - Observations describe evidence before assigning cause.

3. **Measure**
   - At least four useful measurements or records.
   - Measurement location, time, method, sample identity, and units must be explicit when relevant.

4. **Mechanism**
   - The literature must explain enough underlying biology or engineering to make the decision understandable and transferable.

5. **Decide**
   - At least four decision rules.
   - Rules should state when evidence is insufficient and when escalation or additional measurement is required.

6. **Verify**
   - The learner must know how to check whether a change worked.
   - Verification should use comparable observations or measurements rather than memory.

7. **Common failure modes**
   - Misconceptions, confounders, measurement errors, sampling problems, and over-generalization must be visible rather than hidden.

8. **Go deeper**
   - Every major topic should connect to at least two relevant tools, diagnostics, courses, encyclopedia entries, atlases, or tracking workflows.

## Visual standard

A visual is instructional only when it has a defined learning job. Each major topic must identify at least three useful visual roles such as:
- anatomy or system diagram;
- comparison plate;
- sampling or sensor placement map;
- decision tree;
- before/after process;
- workflow;
- timeline;
- measurement geometry;
- diagnostic differential.

Decorative cannabis imagery does not count toward instructional visual coverage.

## Evidence standard

THC content must:
- distinguish observation, measurement, interpretation, and causal claim;
- identify transfer limits when evidence comes from another crop, environment, cultivar, scale, or population;
- separate source-reported lineage or claims from independently measured evidence;
- avoid universal numerical prescriptions unless supported for the stated context;
- make current legal, pesticide-label, electrical, fire, worker-safety, and equipment boundaries explicit where relevant;
- preserve uncertainty instead of filling gaps with confident language.

## Discovery standard

A learner should be able to enter the education system in four ways:

- **By stage** — lifecycle and production sequence.
- **By problem** — symptom, failure, or diagnostic question.
- **By system** — plant, environment, root zone, light, nutrition, health, postharvest, genetics, research.
- **By skill** — measuring, scouting, mapping, propagating, training, diagnosing, recording, analyzing, and verifying.

All four paths should converge on the same canonical knowledge instead of creating contradictory duplicate articles.

## Depth model

Use progressive disclosure:

**Level 1 — orientation**
Plain-language answer and the next useful action.

**Level 2 — field guide**
Observe, measure, decide, verify.

**Level 3 — mechanism**
Plant science, engineering, chemistry, pathology, genetics, or measurement principles.

**Level 4 — professional application**
SOPs, forms, calculations, quality systems, diagnostics, and scenario work.

**Level 5 — evidence**
Primary/official sources, applicability limits, claim control, and revision history.

## Quality gate

A topic is not complete because it has many paragraphs. It is complete only when the learner can:
- identify the problem or objective;
- collect useful evidence;
- interpret it without common category errors;
- use a relevant tool or procedure;
- make a bounded decision;
- verify the outcome;
- understand what remains uncertain;
- navigate to the next appropriate depth.

The repository validator should fail when these required layers are missing.
