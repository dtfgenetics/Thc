# Root-zone evidence protocol — flowering soil case

**Scope:** Educational supplement to `DX-CASE-SOIL-EC-01`. This is a worked evidence collection protocol, not a remote diagnosis or fertilizer prescription.

## Why this case is difficult

An 11 L soil container, weeks 5–7 of flowering, visibly yellow leaves with brown margins, reported elevated pH, frequent heavy feeding, and **unknown root-zone EC** can support several simultaneous explanations. Tip necrosis and chlorosis alone cannot distinguish high soluble salts from impaired aeration, actual elemental shortage, or multiple stressors.

## Evidence priority

| Priority | Observation / measurement | Required metadata | What it separates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Soil/root-zone pH | Numeric value, calibrated meter, sample location, sampling or extraction method, date | Tests whether the claimed high pH is repeatable; not a universal lockout threshold |
| 2 | Soil/root-zone EC | mS/cm, same extraction method every time, calibration, temperature or meter compensation | Tests high soluble-salt hypothesis; EC does not determine which ions are present |
| 3 | Input water/fertilizer EC and pH | Before/after mixing, dose, volume, units, time | Separates high input concentration from later accumulation |
| 4 | Moisture and drainage | Pot mass trend, substrate moisture method, drainage behavior | Tests saturation/dryback and oxygen limitation |
| 5 | Root observation | Location, odor, texture, representative photos; avoid destructive overinterpretation | Supports or weakens root-health hypothesis |
| 6 | Leaf progression | Same angle and lighting, leaf age and position, date-stamped images | Separates progressive injury from stable old damage |

### Sample comparability rules

1. **Do not equate runoff EC with a standardized soil extraction EC.** Runoff is method- and timing-dependent. Record which method was used.
2. **Do not infer a missing nutrient solely from the EC number.** EC measures bulk conductivity, not ion composition.
3. **Do not call pH-related “lockout” confirmed from a single reading.** Assess pH together with elemental availability, tissue analyses when warranted, and the plant's progression.
4. **Do not treat a response to one intervention as proof of one mechanism.** More than one variable may have changed.
5. **Do not use old necrotic leaves as a recovery endpoint.** They do not regenerate; track new injury and the rate of spread.

## Differential checkpoints

**Soluble-salt stress:** gains weight with sustained elevated comparable soil-extract EC plus high input records and compatible injury. Loses weight when repeated comparable EC readings are appropriate and another measured cause accounts for progression.

**pH-associated availability:** gains weight with repeatably elevated soil pH by a validated method and corroborating nutrient evidence. Loses weight with appropriate pH and no availability abnormality. High pH is not synonymous with potassium or magnesium deficiency.

**Water/root-zone dysfunction:** gains weight with persistently saturated media, poor aeration or unhealthy roots. Loses weight with documented moisture cycling, good drainage and healthy roots.

**Elemental deficiency:** gains weight from an age-distribution pattern plus feed, substrate or tissue evidence. Loses weight when supplied nutrient is adequate and another directly measured stress explains injury.

## Photo evidence plan

- **Overview image:** Whole canopy, neutral white balance, consistent perspective. Mark oldest and newest symptomatic leaves separately.
- **Mature leaf close-up:** Show marginal necrosis and interveinal pattern without cropping away the leaf base.
- **Young leaf close-up:** Show whether new tissue is affected; avoid interpreting artificial camera color casts.
- **Substrate/container:** Photograph drainage conditions and irrigation hardware, not just the upper soil surface.
- **Optional root evidence:** Document only during normal inspection; distinguish discoloration from decay with other observations.

**Image integrity:** Do not create a fabricated “before” photograph or label a synthetic reference image as the user's plant. Diagrams must be labeled illustrative and include alternative explanations.

## Interpretation template

- **Observed:** Describe specific leaves, positions, and change over time.
- **Reported:** Separate grower-provided pH and fertilizer history from independently measured data.
- **Unknown:** Numerical pH, soil-extract EC, nutrient formulation, watering volume, and actual root health remain unknown in this case.
- **Ranked hypotheses:** Prefer explicit evidence-for, evidence-against, and missing measurement for each.
- **Next decision:** Choose the measurement with the greatest discrimination before adding another intervention.
- **Follow-up:** Record new injury, stable existing lesions, environment, and measurement method.
