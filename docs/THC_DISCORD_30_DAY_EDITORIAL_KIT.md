## Seven 20–40-second short-video production scripts
**Production format for each:** vertical 9:16, accessible burned-in captions, brand opening 1–2 seconds, observation/demo illustration, one useful takeaway, final source credit plus invite/landing-page CTA. Any scientific diagrams and visuals must be licensed or original; verify visible scientific labels. Avoid promising diagnoses from photos.

1. **Why leaf color alone cannot diagnose a problem.** Hook: "Yellow leaves don't automatically mean a nutrient deficiency." Voiceover: "A changing leaf can reflect multiple causes. Before naming one, record which leaves changed first, the growth stage, recent irrigation, root-zone pH if measured, and the environment. Compare patterns over time. A good diagnosis is a process of ruling possibilities in and out." On-screen: leaf placement; environmental log; timeline. Question: "What would you measure first?"
2. **VPD versus relative humidity.** Hook: "Same humidity, different plant demand." Voiceover: "Relative humidity is a fraction of how much water vapor air holds at its temperature. Vapor pressure deficit describes the gap between actual vapor pressure and saturation pressure. Temperature changes that gap—even with identical relative humidity. Leaf-to-air VPD may differ from air VPD when leaf temperature differs. Know which you're measuring." On-screen: two temperatures at one RH, labeled *conceptual* rather than fabricated values. Question: "Do you record leaf temperature?"
3. **PPFD and DLI aren't the same measurement.** Hook: "Intensity and daily total are different." Voiceover: "PPFD describes a photon flux density at an instant. Daily light integral is the total photosynthetic photons delivered per unit area during a day. If PPFD is roughly constant, DLI equals PPFD times hours of lighting times 0.0036. Changes in duration affect total delivery even if the intensity stays the same." On-screen: `DLI (mol/m²/day) = PPFD (µmol/m²/s) × hours × 0.0036`. Question: "Do you track both?"
4. **What root-zone pH can and can't tell you.** Hook: "A single pH target does not fit every medium." Voiceover: "pH influences nutrient chemistry and potential availability, but a reading only has meaning with sample method, growth medium, fertilizer, and water context. Hydroponic solution and field soil are not interchangeable. Record where you sampled and how you calibrated before interpreting the result." On-screen: sample → calibrated meter → media context. Question: "Which medium are you measuring?"
5. **Genes aren't a guaranteed visual outcome.** Hook: "Same genetics can still look different." Voiceover: "Genotype is the genetic information an organism carries. Phenotype is the set of observed characteristics that develops through interactions between genotype and environment. Different growing conditions can alter measurable traits without changing the inherited genotype. When you compare plants, document environmental differences too." On-screen: genotype + environment → phenotype (conceptual model). Question: "Which environmental variable would you standardize?"
6. **Why roots need oxygen.** Hook: "Roots need more than water." Voiceover: "Living roots respire: their cells use oxygen to release usable energy from stored or supplied compounds. Waterlogged media can slow oxygen movement through pore spaces and stress roots. Root health depends on the interactions among water, air-filled pore space, temperature, and biology—not simply irrigation volume." On-screen: air-filled vs water-filled pore diagram; never claim all wet roots are diseased. Question: "How do you document irrigation and drainage?"
7. **Observe, compare, verify.** Hook: "A photo is the beginning of a diagnosis—not the end." Voiceover: "Good plant science starts with repeatable observations. Capture consistent photos, record location and stage, collect environmental readings, list plausible explanations, and test the most informative next observation. The goal is better evidence, not false certainty." On-screen: observe → differential → check → follow-up. Question: "What one measurement is missing in most online plant-health posts?"

## Educational challenge #1
**Title:** Seven-Day Observation Notebook.
**Prompt:** For seven days, document one plant or permitted household garden specimen with consistent photo angle, date, lighting context, visible changes and one environmental measurement. Do not post home locations or identifying information; you can participate using a hypothetical example or non-cannabis plant. On the last day, share what changed and one uncertainty. No competition by yield or regulated product.
**Scoring rubric:** evidence completeness 40%, clarity 25%, uncertainty 20%, constructive peer feedback 15%. No product rewards promised.
