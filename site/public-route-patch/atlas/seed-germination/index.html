Seed & Germination

A seed contains the complete genetic blueprint plus stored energy to sustain the embryo until photosynthesis begins. Understanding germination biology reduces failure rates and improves early establishment — the foundation of every run.

Core rule

Germination fails at the extremes, not the middle.

Cannabis seeds germinate reliably within a narrow temperature (22–26°C), moisture (damp but not saturated), and oxygen band. The most common germination failures — damping off, no radicle emergence, slow emergence — all trace to one of these three factors being outside range, not seed quality alone.

Viability check

Assess before you germinate

Mature, viable seeds are dark (brown, black, or tiger-striped), firm, and waxy-shelled. Pale, green, soft, or cracked seeds have lower viability. A water float test (24h) is a rough proxy — viable seeds typically sink, but this is not absolute and waterlogging can damage some viable seeds.

Seed anatomy

What is inside a cannabis seed

anatomy

Seed Coat (Testa)

The hard outer shell — protects the embryo from mechanical damage, water, and pathogens. A waxy, intact testa with no cracks or soft spots is the first viability indicator. Damage to the testa accelerates desiccation and pathogen entry.

anatomy

Endosperm

Stored energy tissue surrounding the embryo. Provides the carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins that sustain the seedling from germination until cotyledons are photosynthetically active. Endosperm quality directly affects germination speed and seedling vigour.

anatomy

Embryo

The miniature plant inside the seed: radicle (first root), hypocotyl (stem below cotyledons), cotyledons (seed leaves), and plumule (the first true leaf bud). All are present in dormancy, waiting for the correct germination trigger.

anatomy

Radicle

The embryonic root — the first structure to emerge during germination. It breaks through the testa and grows downward (geotropism). Radicle length at transplant ideally 3–5mm for paper-towel methods; roots should be white and undamaged.

Development sequence

From dormant seed to established seedling

Imbibition

Seed absorbs water, activating enzymatic processes. Testa softens. Temperature 22–26°C is critical here.

Radicle emergence

Root tip breaks through testa, begins growing downward. First visible sign of germination. 24–72h in optimal conditions.

Hypocotyl elongation

Stem below the cotyledons extends, pushing the seed head up through the medium.

Cotyledon emergence

Seed leaves unfold and expand. Photosynthesis begins. The seedling is no longer endosperm-dependent.

First true leaves

Single-blade leaves appear from the plumule. Root system is establishing below. Lowest nutrient demand of any stage.

Established seedling

3–5 node development, expanding root mass, transition to vegetative feeding programme.

Common germination failures

  • No emergence in 7+ days: temperature below 20°C, medium too wet (oxygen excluded), or non-viable seed
  • Radicle but no shoot: too deep in medium, or medium crusted over — seedling can't push through
  • Damping off: stem rot at soil line — too wet, poor airflow, or pathogen pressure (Pythium, Fusarium)
  • Stretching seedling: insufficient light intensity after emergence — not a germination failure, a light issue
  • Cotyledons stay closed: seed coat stuck on cotyledons from insufficient moisture during emergence — gently moisten, do not force
