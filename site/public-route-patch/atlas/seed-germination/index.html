Development Deep Lab · Germination

Germination is a sequence, not a pass-or-fail moment.

Track the seed from water uptake through metabolic reactivation, radicle emergence, cotyledon opening, and early seedling establishment. When emergence fails, identify where the sequence stopped before assigning a cause.

Anatomy

Seed coat, embryo, cotyledons

The seed protects a living embryo and stored reserves before favorable conditions support renewed growth. Radicle and shoot-axis development establish the first root and shoot systems.

EmbryoCotyledonRadicleHypocotyl
Process

Imbibition → emergence → establishment

Imbibition: water uptake begins the transition out of quiescence.
Metabolic restart: respiration and reserve mobilization increase.
Radicle emergence: the primary root axis exits first.
Cotyledon opening: the young shoot transitions toward photosynthetic growth.
Measurements

Record conditions through time

  • Seed condition and storage history.
  • Imbibition time and time to radicle emergence.
  • Root-zone temperature and moisture consistency.
  • Time to cotyledon opening and first true-leaf development.
Differential

Failure does not identify one cause

Seed vigor, viability, moisture, oxygen, temperature, handling, and time can all affect emergence or establishment.

Evidence boundary: a shell remaining attached or a seed failing to emerge is not, by itself, proof of one environmental error.
