Core rule

Germination fails at the extremes, not the middle.

Cannabis seeds germinate reliably within a narrow temperature (22–26°C), moisture, and oxygen band. The most common failures — damping off, no radicle emergence, slow emergence — all trace to one of these three factors being outside range, not seed quality alone.

Viability check

Assess before you germinate.

Mature viable seeds are dark (brown, black, or tiger-striped), firm, and waxy-shelled. Pale, green, soft, or cracked seeds have lower viability. A water float test (24h) is a rough proxy — viable seeds typically sink — but waterlogging can damage some viable seeds.

Seed anatomy

What is inside a cannabis seed and what each structure does.

anatomy

Seed Coat (Testa)

The hard outer shell. Protects the embryo from mechanical damage, water, and pathogens. A waxy, intact testa with no cracks or soft spots is the first viability indicator.

anatomy

Endosperm

Stored energy tissue surrounding the embryo. Provides the carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins that sustain the seedling from germination until cotyledons are photosynthetically active.

anatomy

Embryo

The miniature plant inside the seed: radicle (first root), hypocotyl (stem below cotyledons), cotyledons (seed leaves), and plumule (first true leaf bud). All present in dormancy.

anatomy

Radicle

The embryonic root — the first structure to emerge. Breaks through the testa and grows downward (geotropism). Ideally 3–5mm at transplant, white and undamaged.

Development sequence

From dormant seed to established seedling — six stages.

Imbibition

Seed absorbs water, activating enzymatic processes. Testa softens. Temperature 22–26°C is critical.

Radicle emergence

Root tip breaks through testa, grows downward. First visible sign of germination — 24–72h in optimal conditions.

Hypocotyl elongation

Stem below the cotyledons extends, pushing the seed head up through the medium.

Cotyledon emergence

Seed leaves unfold and expand. Photosynthesis begins. The seedling is no longer endosperm-dependent.

First true leaves

Single-blade leaves appear from the plumule. Root system establishing. Lowest nutrient demand of any stage.

Established seedling

3–5 node development, expanding root mass, transition to vegetative feeding programme.

Failure modes & environment

Common failures

Why germination fails.

  • No emergence in 7+ days: temperature below 20°C, medium too wet (oxygen excluded), or non-viable seed
  • Radicle but no shoot: too deep in medium, or medium crusted over — seedling can't push through
  • Damping off: stem rot at soil line — too wet, poor airflow, or pathogen pressure (Pythium, Fusarium)
  • Stretching seedling: insufficient light intensity after emergence — not a germination failure
  • Cotyledons stay closed: seed coat stuck — gently moisten, do not force open
