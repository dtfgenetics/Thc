Root Zone Problem Card

Systematically identify the source of root zone problems before acting. pH, EC, temperature, oxygen, and root inspection each point to different causes requiring different responses.

All Field Cards Diagnostics Protocol
Core rule

Diagnose the root zone before treating the canopy.

The majority of foliar symptoms originate in the root zone. A plant showing yellowing, wilting, or slow growth that does not respond to foliar interventions almost certainly has a root zone problem. Diagnose from the root up — not from the leaf down.

Measure in sequence

pH → EC → temperature → oxygen → root inspection.

Each measurement either confirms or rules out the next step. Starting with root inspection before measuring pH, EC, and temperature misses the most common causes and risks contaminating the root system during inspection.

3Root zone temperature Above 26°C: pythium risk; Below 18°C: uptake failure

6Root colour Brown/grey: oxygen stress or pathogen; Black/slimy: active pythium or fusarium
7Root smell Fishy or rotten: active anaerobic pathogen

Response guide

Root rot confirmed

Immediate response sequence.

  1. Improve drainage — remove obstruction or increase runoff openings
  2. Reduce irrigation frequency immediately — allow extended dryback
  3. Reduce root zone temperature below 22°C
  4. Increase aeration — air stones in hydro; substrate amendment in coco/soil
  5. Remove and isolate heavily infected plants
  6. Avoid hydrogen peroxide — sterilises beneficial microbes that suppress pythium
  7. Consider Trichoderma and Bacillus subtilis after environment is corrected
Salt accumulation confirmed

Flush protocol.

  1. Irrigate with plain water adjusted to correct pH
  2. Volume: 2–3× container volume minimum
  3. Collect and measure runoff EC at the end of the flush
  4. Target runoff EC: within 0.5 mS/cm of input water EC
  5. Allow 20–30% dryback before resuming nutrient feed
  6. Resume at 50–75% of previous EC — build back up over 2–3 feeds
