Deficiency vs Lockout Comparison Card

Determine whether a deficiency symptom is caused by actual absence of the nutrient (true deficiency) or pH-driven unavailability (lockout). The correct response differs completely.

All Field Cards Diagnostics Protocol
Core rule

Most apparent deficiencies are actually lockout — not absence.

The nutrient is in the substrate. The pH is preventing it from being absorbed. Adding more of a locked-out nutrient at the wrong pH changes nothing except the EC level. Fix the pH first and give the plant 3–5 days to respond before adding any element.

Response test

True deficiency responds to adding the element. Lockout does not.

If you correct pH and symptoms continue after 5–7 days, the deficiency may be genuine. If symptoms resolve after pH correction without adding the element, it was lockout. Never add nutrients and fix pH simultaneously — you cannot know which change caused the improvement.

Response protocol

Correct sequence.

  1. Correct pH to the middle of the target range
  2. Wait 3–5 days — do NOT add any nutrient yet
  3. Observe whether symptoms are stabilising
  4. If pH was the issue, recovery begins within 5–7 days
  5. If symptoms continue after correct pH for 1 week, check whether the element is actually in the feed at sufficient concentration
  6. Only add the specific element after ruling out lockout
