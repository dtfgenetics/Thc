Necrosis Evaluation Card

Evaluate dead leaf tissue by pattern, location, and edge characteristics. The boundary between necrotic and living tissue is more informative than the dead tissue itself.

All Field Cards Diagnostics Protocol
Core rule

The edge of the necrosis tells you more than the dead tissue.

A sharp yellow halo around dead tissue suggests pathogen involvement. A gradual fade from yellow to brown suggests nutritional or environmental cause. An abrupt change with no halo suggests contact damage or intense heat.

Key question

Is it spreading or stable?

Active spreading necrosis with a living yellow border indicates an ongoing cause — pathogen or progressing deficiency. Stable necrosis is residual from a past event and does not require the same urgency of response.

Necrosis pattern checklist

MarkPatternLocationMost likely cause
□Margin / edge necrosis — brown edges, tipsOld leavesPotassium deficiency, salt accumulation, excess heat at edge
□Tip necrosis — brown tips on new growthNewest leaves, growing tipCalcium deficiency from low VPD or root damage; boron deficiency
□Brown spots with yellow haloAny positionFungal or bacterial pathogen (Botrytis, septoria, bacterial leaf spot)
□Tan/brown spots, no halo, sharp edgeUpper canopyLight burn, heat contact, pesticide phytotoxicity
□Large irregular brown areas, spreadingMid-canopy, multiple leavesRoot rot (pythium/fusarium) — systemic vascular failure
□Tiny dark pin-point spotsScattered — upper canopyThrip feeding damage, russet mite scarring, late-stage spider mite
□Brown interveinal patchesOld leavesManganese toxicity or iron toxicity (very low pH)

Assessment fields

Observation

Record before acting.

  • □ Is necrosis spreading actively or stable?
  • □ Does it have a yellow/green border?
  • □ Are other plants affected?
  • □ Which canopy position — upper, mid, or lower?
  • □ Old growth, new growth, or both?
  • □ Checked leaf underside with 60× loupe?
  • □ Root condition checked?
Do not

Before diagnosing.

  • □ Do not remove affected leaves without diagnosing — remove evidence only after photographing
  • □ Do not spray fungicide on physically burned tissue — adds damage
  • □ Do not treat for potassium without checking pH and EC — marginal necrosis has many causes
  • □ Do not conclude calcium without checking VPD — tip necrosis is often transpiration failure
