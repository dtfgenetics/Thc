Leaf Diagnostic Field Card

A structured observation card for any leaf symptom. Use this before diagnosing — description and measurement first, cause second.

All Field Cards Diagnostics Protocol
Observation rule

Describe what you see before naming a cause.

Location, distribution, pattern, affected tissue age, and progression speed are the diagnostic signal — not the overall colour or shape of the damage. Write down what you observe before considering what caused it.

First question

Old growth or new growth?

Mobile nutrients (N, Mg, K, P, S, Mo) deficiency appears on old leaves first. Immobile nutrients (Ca, Fe, Mn, B, Cu, Zn) always show on new growth first. This single observation halves the list of possible causes immediately.

Observation checklist

Step 1 — Describe the pattern

What does it look like?

  • □ Uniform yellowing — whole leaf fading evenly
  • □ Interveinal yellowing — green veins, yellow between
  • □ Marginal yellowing — edges yellow, centre green
  • □ Tip yellowing — tip or tips only
  • □ Brown spots — with or without yellow halo
  • □ Marginal necrosis — brown leaf edges
  • □ Tip necrosis — brown tips only
  • □ Leaf curl up — edges curl upward
  • □ Leaf curl down — edges or tips curl down
  • □ Stippling — tiny dots (mite damage)
  • □ Silver streaking — thrip feeding scars
  • □ Distorted new growth — pest or Ca/B deficiency
Step 2 — Map the distribution

Where is it?

  • □ Old leaves only — bottom of plant
  • □ New growth only — top, growing tip
  • □ Whole plant — uniform throughout
  • □ One branch — isolated location
  • □ One side of the canopy — directional
  • □ Upper canopy — closest to light
  • □ Lower canopy — light-excluded zone
  • □ Multiple plants in the room
  • □ Single plant only
  • □ Spreading — getting worse
  • □ Stable — not progressing

Before acting — rule out these first

Root zone pH

The most common cause of deficiency symptoms. Check before adding any nutrient.

Root health

Inspect root colour and smell. Root damage prevents uptake of everything regardless of substrate levels.

VPD / environment

Tip burn and new growth issues are often transpiration-driven, not nutritional.

Pest inspection

60× loupe on leaf undersides. Rule out mites and thrips before concluding nutritional cause.
