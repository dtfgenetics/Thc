THC-C007 · Leaf Anatomy & Gas Exchange · Teaching Healthy Cultivation

Step 1 — Describe what you see

Use neutral language. Write what a photograph would show — not what you think caused it.

✓Pattern observedNotes
☐Uniform yellowing — whole leaf fading evenly
☐Interveinal yellowing — green veins, yellow tissue betweenOld or new growth?
☐Marginal yellowing — edges yellow, centre green
☐Tip yellowing — tip only, or tips of leaflets
☐Brown spots — with or without yellow haloSharp or gradual edge?
☐Marginal necrosis — brown leaf edges, crispy
☐Tip necrosis — brown tips on new growth
☐Upward curl — edges curl toward the light (taco)Whole plant or tip only?
☐Downward curl — tips or edges curl down (claw)
☐Stippling — tiny white or bronze dots on surfaceCheck underside with loupe
☐Silver streaking — metallic lines on upper surfaceLikely thrip damage
☐New growth distortion — cupped, thickened, twistedInspect with 60× loupe

Step 2 — Map the distribution

✓Location
☐Old leaves only — bottom of plant first → mobile nutrient
☐New growth only — growing tip and uppermost leaves → immobile nutrient or pest
☐Whole plant uniformly — systemic cause or light issue
☐One branch / one side — localised vascular or environmental cause
☐Upper canopy — heat, light, or VPD stress
☐Lower canopy only — light exclusion or normal senescence
☐All plants in the room → environmental or nutritional cause
☐One plant only → root zone or pest problem specific to that plant

Step 4 — Rule out these before acting

pH first

Most deficiency symptoms are pH lockout. Test before adding any nutrient.

Root health

Inspect root colour and smell. Root damage disrupts uptake of everything.

VPD / environment

Tip burn and new growth curl are often transpiration-driven, not nutritional.

Pest inspection

60× loupe on leaf undersides. Rule out mites and thrips first.
