Watering & Dryback Field Card

Make irrigation decisions based on container weight and dryback percentage — not visual inspection or fixed schedules. This card guides the correct measurement approach.

All Field Cards Diagnostics Protocol
Core rule

Irrigate by container weight — not by schedule or visual inspection.

Plant water demand varies with VPD, light intensity, growth stage, and temperature. A fixed daily schedule ignores all of these. Container weight at a consistent measuring time is the only reliable indicator of how much the plant has transpired and how close it is to the dryback target.

Dryback delivers oxygen

Controlled drying is the primary mechanism for root zone aeration.

As substrate dries, air re-enters the pore space previously filled with water. This oxygen delivery to roots is essential for root function and prevents the anaerobic conditions that enable pythium. Dryback is not neglect — it is management.

Dryback Lab Tool

Container weight protocol

Set field capacity

Irrigate until runoff. Weigh immediately after drainage stops. Record as your 100% reference weight.

Set dry reference

Weigh the empty container + dry substrate. This is your 0% reference. Keep consistent across transplants.

Calculate target

Target weight = dry weight + (field capacity − dry weight) × (1 − dryback%). Example: 20% dryback = 80% of water capacity remaining.

Irrigate at target

When container reaches target weight, irrigate. Return to field capacity in one shot or multiple smaller shots spaced 15–30 min apart.

Track the cycle

Note time from field capacity to target dryback. This tells you plant demand rate — increasing in flower, decreasing under stress.

Adjust by stage

Shorten dryback as flower demand increases. Lengthen at the end of the run during the ripening/late-flower phase.
