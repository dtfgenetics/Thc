Leaf Anatomy

Leaf anatomy is the baseline reference for every visual diagnosis. Before deciding what is wrong, identify what a healthy leaf is supposed to contain and how each structure supports plant function.

AtlasLeaf ModuleNext: Photosynthesis

Core structures

  • Blade: broad light-capturing surface.
  • Petiole: stalk connecting blade to stem.
  • Midrib and veins: transport water, minerals, sugars, and signals.
  • Leaflets and serrated margins: increase surface area and affect heat exchange.
  • Upper and lower epidermis: protective surfaces with different stomatal density.

Why it matters

Symptoms often follow anatomy. Interveinal chlorosis follows the space between veins. Marginal necrosis begins at leaf edges. Tip burn begins where water and dissolved salts concentrate. Pest damage often appears on the underside or along veins.

Observe first

  • Is the symptom on old growth, new growth, or both?
  • Does it follow veins, edges, tips, or random spots?
  • Is the underside clean or showing eggs, webbing, frass, or mites?
  • Are leaves flat, cupped, clawed, twisted, or drooping?
