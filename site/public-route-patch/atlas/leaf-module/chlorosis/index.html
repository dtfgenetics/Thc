Chlorosis

Chlorosis is yellowing from reduced chlorophyll. It is a symptom category, not a diagnosis. Pattern and location are the primary diagnostic tools — the overall colour tells you almost nothing on its own.

← TranspirationModule IndexNecrosis →
Core rule

Pattern and location are the diagnosis — not the colour.

Uniform yellowing, interveinal yellowing on old leaves, and pale new growth with green veins are three completely different situations with different causes. Describing the pattern precisely before naming a cause is the single most important step.

Mobile vs immobile

Old tissue vs new tissue narrows the list by half.

Mobile nutrients (N, Mg, K, P, S, Mo) are reallocated from old to new growth — deficiency appears on older leaves. Immobile (Ca, Fe, Mn, B, Cu, Zn) cannot be moved — deficiency appears on new growth first.

What mimics chlorosis

look-alike

Normal senescence

Old fan leaves yellowing in late flower is expected nitrogen mobilisation. Slow, bottom-up progression in an otherwise healthy plant — not a deficiency emergency.

look-alike

Light deficiency

Leaves in shaded canopy areas lose chlorophyll — the plant downregulates capacity where it is not needed. Check PPFD at the yellowing site before treating nutritionally.

look-alike

pH lockout

Most nutrient deficiency chlorosis symptoms are actually pH putting that nutrient out of availability range — not actual absence. Test root-zone pH before adding any element.

Observation sequence

Before acting

The correct sequence.

  1. Note where yellowing appears: old or new tissue, which canopy position
  2. Describe pattern: uniform, interveinal, marginal, blotchy, or tip-to-centre progression
  3. Check if green veins are preserved in the yellowed area
  4. Measure root-zone pH and EC
  5. Inspect roots and irrigation history
  6. Check VPD and environmental log for recent changes
  7. Build a cause list — test most likely with the smallest intervention
Common errors

What to avoid.

  • Pattern-matching to deficiency pictures without confirming pH — adding iron at 7.0 does nothing
  • Adding nitrogen for any general yellowing without checking canopy position first
  • Not checking roots before treating foliage — root damage disrupts uptake of everything
  • Treating normal senescence as an emergency
  • Making multiple adjustments at once — impossible to know what worked
