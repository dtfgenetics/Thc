Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light energy into glucose through two stages in the chloroplast. Its rate is always limited by the scarcest input — and adding more of a non-limiting factor produces almost no response.

← Leaf AnatomyModule IndexModule IndexStomata →
Core rule

Always limited by the scarcest input — not the most abundant.

Light, CO₂, water, and temperature all feed into the rate. The one in shortest supply sets the ceiling. Adding more of a non-limiting input produces almost no response.

Grower implication

Match your inputs to each other before adding more of anything.

CO₂ supplementation at 400 μmol/m²/s PPFD does almost nothing. Raising PPFD without adequate CO₂ above ~800 μmol hits the same ceiling. Identify the limit first, then raise all inputs together.

Photosynthesis and canopy science

THC-ENC-072 · Leaf Area, Canopy Photosynthesis & Self-Shading · Teaching Healthy Cultivation

Two stages

Light reactions — Thylakoids

Light energy → ATP + NADPH

  • Photosystem II: absorbs red (680nm) and blue (450nm) light; splits water, releases O₂
  • Electron transport chain: electrons move from PSII to PSI, generating ATP
  • Photosystem I: uses ATP to generate NADPH (reducing power for stage 2)
  • Oxygen released as byproduct of water splitting at PSII
Calvin Cycle — Stroma

CO₂ + ATP + NADPH → Glucose

  • Carbon fixation: CO₂ binds to RuBP via Rubisco — the most abundant enzyme on Earth
  • Reduction: ATP and NADPH convert fixed carbon into G3P
  • Regeneration: G3P regenerates RuBP and produces glucose
  • 6 CO₂ molecules fixed per glucose molecule produced
