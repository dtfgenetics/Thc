Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis converts light into chemical energy stored as glucose. Its rate determines growth potential — and the limiting factor at any moment determines where to focus first.

← Leaf AnatomyModule IndexStomata →
Core rule

Photosynthesis is always limited by its scarcest input.

Light, CO₂, water, and temperature all feed into the rate. The one in shortest supply sets the ceiling. Adding more of non-limiting inputs produces almost no response.

Grower implication

Match your inputs to each other — do not outrun a limiting factor.

CO₂ supplementation at 400 μmol/m²/s PPFD does almost nothing. Raising PPFD without adequate CO₂ above ~800 μmol hits the same ceiling in reverse. Identify the limit first.

Two stages

Light reactions — Thylakoids

Light energy → ATP + NADPH

  • Photosystem II: absorbs red (680nm) and blue (450nm) light; splits water, releases O₂, energises electrons
  • Electron transport chain: electrons move from PSII to PSI, generating ATP
  • Photosystem I: uses ATP to generate NADPH (reducing power for stage 2)
  • Oxygen released as a byproduct of water splitting
Calvin Cycle — Stroma

CO₂ + ATP + NADPH → Glucose

  • Carbon fixation: CO₂ binds to RuBP via Rubisco enzyme — the most abundant enzyme on Earth
  • Reduction: ATP and NADPH convert fixed carbon into G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate)
  • Regeneration: G3P regenerates RuBP for the next cycle and produces glucose
  • 6 CO₂ molecules are fixed per glucose molecule produced
