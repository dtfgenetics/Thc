Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is how leaves convert light energy, carbon dioxide, and water into sugars that support growth, roots, flowers, defense, and recovery.

PreviousLeaf ModuleNext

Inputs

  • Light intensity and spectrum.
  • Carbon dioxide entering through stomata.
  • Water supplied by the root system.
  • Minerals needed for chlorophyll, enzymes, and transport.

Outputs

The plant produces sugars and oxygen. Sugars move through the phloem to growing tips, roots, flowers, and storage tissues. Weak photosynthesis can show as slow growth before obvious damage appears.

Limiting factors

More light does not help if CO2, water movement, temperature, nutrient status, or root health is limiting. The Atlas treats photosynthesis as a whole-plant process, not only a light number.
