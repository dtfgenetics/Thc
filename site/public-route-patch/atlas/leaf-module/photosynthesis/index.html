Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis captures light energy and fixes carbon into carbohydrates through linked reactions. Light, carbon dioxide, temperature, water status and plant physiology interact; none alone explains every change in growth.

← Leaf AnatomyModule IndexModule IndexStomata →
Core rule

Identify interacting constraints before changing inputs.

Light response, CO₂ supply, temperature and stomatal behavior interact. Measure local conditions and plant response rather than assuming a single universal limit.

Grower implication

Match your inputs to each other before adding more of anything.

There is no universal PPFD threshold at which supplemental CO₂ becomes useful. Responses depend on cultivar, temperature, canopy, ventilation and baseline CO₂. Change and document one variable at a time.

Photosynthesis: connected processes

Original scientific teaching schematic · THC — Teaching Healthy Cultivation

Two stages

Light reactions — Thylakoids

Light energy → ATP + NADPH

  • Photosystem II: absorbs red (680nm) and blue (450nm) light; splits water, releases O₂
  • Electron transport chain: electron flow supports a proton gradient used by ATP synthase
  • Photosystem I: transfers light-excited electrons toward NADP⁺ reduction to NADPH
  • Oxygen released as byproduct of water splitting at PSII
Calvin Cycle — Stroma

CO₂ + ATP + NADPH → Glucose

  • Carbon fixation: CO₂ binds to RuBP via Rubisco — the most abundant enzyme on Earth
  • Reduction: ATP and NADPH convert fixed carbon into G3P
  • Regeneration: much of the triose phosphate regenerates RuBP; some contributes to carbohydrate synthesis
  • Net fixation of six CO₂ can supply carbon equivalent to one hexose; production and allocation depend on metabolism
