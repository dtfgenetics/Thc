Leaf Curl

Leaf curl is the leaf's physical response to stress — but the direction, extent, and affected tissue age all point to different causes. Curl direction is the first observation to make.

← NecrosisModule IndexNutrient Symptoms →
Core rule

Curl direction is the first diagnostic signal.

Upward curl (taco, prayer) and downward curl (clawing) have different causes. Assessing which direction the curl is happening — and whether it affects old or new leaves — narrows the cause substantially before any other measurement.

Time-of-day matters

Curl that appears and disappears with the light cycle is stomatal — not structural.

Temporary upward curl during the high-VPD midday window that recovers toward lights-off is stomatal closure, not a persistent problem. Curl that persists through the dark period suggests a structural or chemical cause.

Assessment

Observation guide

What to note first.

  1. Direction: upward or downward?
  2. Which tissue: new growth, old leaves, or whole plant?
  3. Does it change with the light cycle? Note at lights-on peak and again at lights-off
  4. Check canopy temperature — not room temperature. Leaf temperature at canopy level
  5. Check VPD — upward curl almost always implicates VPD or temperature before nutrition
  6. Use a 60× loupe on new growth curl — broad mite and russet mite cause similar distortion to calcium/boron deficiency
Before supplementing

What to rule out first.

  • Downward claw ≠ nitrogen deficiency: claw almost always means excess nitrogen, overwatering, or root problems — not deficiency
  • New growth curl ≠ calcium deficiency: check VPD and airflow before adding calcium to the root zone
  • Upward taco ≠ water stress: check VPD and temperature before increasing irrigation frequency
  • Whole-plant downward droop: check if leaves feel firm (overwatering) or papery (water deficit) before adjusting anything
