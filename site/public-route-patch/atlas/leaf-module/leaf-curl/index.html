Leaf Curl

Leaf curling is a nonspecific observation. Record direction, affected tissue age, symptom progression, and time-of-day changes; verify root-zone and environmental conditions before attributing a cause.

← NecrosisModule IndexModule IndexNutrient Symptoms →
Core rule

Curl direction is an observation, not a diagnosis.

Upward and downward curling can have overlapping causes. Record direction, tissue age and symptom progression, then confirm temperature, humidity, substrate moisture and root conditions.

Time check

Does it appear during lights-on and recover at lights-off?

Curl that varies with light and environmental conditions may reflect changing water balance or leaf posture; persistence does not identify a unique cause. Check leaf and air temperature, humidity, irrigation and root health.

Document leaf posture before intervention

Leaf posture observation checklist

  • Record whether curling affects leaf tips, margins, or entire leaves.
  • Compare upper and lower canopy, new and mature leaves.
  • Note time of day, temperature, relative humidity, light intensity, irrigation, and airflow.
  • Inspect roots and growing tips; look for pest evidence before treating.
  • Recheck progression after changing one factor at a time.
