Necrosis

Necrosis is irreversible cell death producing brown, black, or tan tissue. Distribution, progression, recent exposures and nearby tissue help guide investigation; no single lesion pattern proves a cause.

← ChlorosisModule IndexModule IndexLeaf Curl →
Core rule

Document lesion margins, distribution and progression.

Halos and sharp margins can occur with multiple biotic and abiotic causes. Compare healthy and affected leaves, look for pest signs and record exposures before making a diagnosis.

Spreading vs stable

Is it progressing or a historical record?

Progressive injury warrants investigation; stable dead tissue can remain after the cause has changed. Track new damage with dated photographs and measurements.

Assessment and response

What to check

Before acting on necrosis.

  1. Is necrosis spreading or stable? Note whether a yellow border is visible
  2. Compare other plants, allowing for exposure, irrigation, cultivar and pest differences
  3. Check canopy position — upper, mid, or lower
  4. Record tissue age; nutrient mobility may help narrow, but does not determine, causes
  5. Inspect both leaf surfaces with appropriate magnification for pests or reproductive structures; negative inspection does not exclude all pests
Do not

Common errors with necrosis.

  • Remove affected leaves without documenting — photograph first, remove after
  • Spray fungicide on physically burned or salt-damaged tissue — adds more damage
  • Treat marginal necrosis as potassium deficiency without checking pH and EC
  • Conclude calcium deficiency from tip injury without checking other nutrient, root-zone and environmental factors
