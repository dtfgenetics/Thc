Necrosis

Necrosis is irreversible cell death producing brown, black, or tan tissue. The edge of the dead tissue and its position in the canopy are more informative than the dead tissue itself.

← ChlorosisModule IndexModule IndexLeaf Curl →
Core rule

The edge of the necrosis is more informative than the dead tissue.

A sharp yellow halo around dead tissue suggests pathogen involvement. A gradual fade from yellow to brown suggests nutritional or environmental cause. An abrupt change with no halo suggests contact damage or heat.

Spreading vs stable

Is it progressing or a historical record?

Actively spreading necrosis with a yellow border indicates an ongoing cause — act quickly. Stable necrosis that is not progressing is residual from a past event and does not require the same urgency.

Assessment and response

What to check

Before acting on necrosis.

  1. Is necrosis spreading or stable? Note whether a yellow border is visible
  2. Are other plants affected? — environmental or nutritional cause if yes; pest or pathogen if isolated
  3. Check canopy position — upper, mid, or lower
  4. Old or new tissue — determines whether mobile or immobile nutrient is involved
  5. Inspect leaf with 60× loupe — rule out pest damage before concluding nutritional
Do not

Common errors with necrosis.

  • Remove affected leaves without documenting — photograph first, remove after
  • Spray fungicide on physically burned or salt-damaged tissue — adds more damage
  • Treat marginal necrosis as potassium deficiency without checking pH and EC
  • Conclude calcium deficiency from tip necrosis without checking VPD — tip burn is often transpiration failure
