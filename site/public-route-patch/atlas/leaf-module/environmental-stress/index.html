Environmental Stress on Leaves

Leaves respond quickly to environment. Light, temperature, humidity, VPD, airflow, water availability, and root oxygen can change posture, color, texture, and growth rate.

PreviousLeaf ModuleBack to Atlas

Pattern language

  • Upper-canopy stress near lights.
  • Edge curl from heat, light, or VPD pressure.
  • Droop from water/root-zone imbalance.
  • Texture changes from wind, humidity, or spray stress.

What to check

Record air temperature, leaf temperature if available, RH, VPD, PPFD, DLI, photoperiod, fan direction, irrigation timing, substrate moisture, and whether symptoms appear in specific microclimates.

Common look-alikes

Heat stress, light stress, high VPD, low VPD, wind stress, overwatering, underwatering, nutrient concentration, root-zone oxygen issues, and pest pressure can overlap.

Image asset needed

Environmental Leaf Stress Checklist: canopy position, light zone, fan path, VPD status, moisture status, and progression over time.
