Environmental Stress

Environmental stress — from heat, cold, wind, excess light, and low humidity — produces leaf damage distributed by canopy position, not leaf age. This distinguishes it from nutritional symptoms.

← Pest DamageModule IndexModule Index
Core rule

Environmental symptoms are distributed by position — not leaf age.

Heat and light stress appear on the uppermost leaves. Cold stress on the lowest. Wind damage is directional. Nutritional stress follows old-vs-new tissue rules regardless of canopy position.

Measure at the symptom site

Canopy temperature and PPFD differ from room sensors.

The environment the plant experiences at the canopy top differs from the room average. Measure at the actual symptom location before concluding a cause.

Misread as nutritional

Common misdiagnoses

Environmental symptoms treated as nutrients.

  • Tip burn (high VPD) → calcium deficiency: adding calcium to root zone does nothing when delivery mechanism (transpiration) is the problem
  • Heat stress margin tanning → potassium deficiency: both cause margin scorch but heat damage appears on uppermost leaves only
  • Light bleaching → sulfur/nitrogen deficiency: bleaching is irreversible tissue destruction — only reducing light intensity helps
  • Cold-induced purple → phosphorus deficiency: warm the root zone and colouration resolves within days
Three-observation check

Separating environmental from nutritional.

  1. Distribution by position, not age: environmental stress is where the stressor is strongest — nutritional stress follows mobility rules regardless of position
  2. Symmetry across the room: environmental stress affects all plants similarly; nutritional stress in one plant points to a root-zone issue specific to that plant
  3. Time of day correlation: heat and VPD stress worsens during lights-on peak and improves toward lights-off; nutrient symptoms do not change with the light cycle
