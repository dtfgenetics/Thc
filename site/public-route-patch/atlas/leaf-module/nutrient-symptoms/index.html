Field checklist

  • Separate old-growth symptoms from new-growth symptoms.
  • Record mobile-vs-immobile style pattern without forcing a diagnosis.
  • Check whether pH/EC or watering explains availability.
  • Compare feed history against plant stage and demand.

Measurements to pair

  • pH, EC/PPM, feed schedule, source water.
  • Media type, irrigation volume, runoff/dryback.
  • Temperature, RH, VPD, and root health.

Common mistakes

  • Adding supplements before measuring basics.
  • Ignoring root stress or lockout-like conditions.
  • Confusing pest damage and environmental stress for nutrients.

Nutrient-like patterns: observations, not diagnoses

Patterns can overlap with watering problems, salinity, pests and environmental stress. Compare affected leaf age with healthy tissue and confirm pH, EC, irrigation and root condition before changing a nutrient program.

Older foliage first

Progressive yellowing or marginal injury can be consistent with mobile-nutrient limitations, natural aging or root-zone stress. Document age, progression and feed history.

Young growth first

Distortion or interveinal paling of newly developing leaves may reflect uptake and transport problems, but also biological or environmental damage. Inspect roots and growing tips.

Across the canopy

Sudden widespread symptoms can follow irrigation, spray, temperature or root-zone changes. Review the timeline before selecting an intervention.

Why leaves can look deficient when nutrients are present

A leaf symptom can follow limited supply, reduced root-zone availability, or reduced uptake and transport. These pathways can overlap; investigate the system rather than treating the diagram as a diagnosis.

Five-step verification workflow

  1. Photograph affected and healthy leaves with plant stage and location.
  2. Record medium, source water, nutrients, irrigation volume and timing.
  3. Measure pH and electrical conductivity with calibrated instruments, using medium-appropriate sampling.
  4. Inspect moisture, root health, pest evidence, temperature, light and humidity.
  5. Change one supported variable, document the change, and monitor new growth rather than expecting dead tissue to recover.

Evidence limit: A visual match does not confirm a deficiency. pH and EC describe conditions but do not independently identify the tissue nutrient concentration.
