Nutrient Symptoms

Nutrient deficiency and toxicity symptoms on leaves follow predictable patterns based on mobility, pH availability, and antagonism between elements. This lesson maps the most common patterns and their differentials.

← Leaf CurlModule IndexPest Damage →
Core rule

Check pH before adding any nutrient.

The vast majority of visual nutrient deficiency symptoms are caused by pH placing an element outside its availability range — not actual absence of that element. Adding more of a locked-out nutrient does not fix the problem and may create antagonism with others.

Mobility first

Old leaves or new leaves determines the element category.

This single observation cuts the differential list in half. Mobile nutrients move to new growth — deficiency appears on old tissue first. Immobile nutrients cannot be relocated — deficiency always shows on new growth first.

Toxicity patterns

Nutrient toxicity signs

Too much is also a problem.

  • Nitrogen toxicity: very dark green leaves, downward claw, excess vegetative growth, soft tissue
  • Phosphorus toxicity: rare; can cause magnesium and zinc lockout through antagonism
  • Potassium toxicity: margin scorch on new growth; blocks Ca and Mg uptake
  • Calcium toxicity: blocks Mg and K; brown spots; high EC; tip necrosis
  • Iron toxicity: bronze/brown leaf discolouration; most common in acidic hydro systems
  • Boron toxicity: margin necrosis on old leaves; very similar to potassium toxicity
Antagonism map

Elements that block each other.

  • High N blocks: calcium and magnesium uptake at root — excess ammonium especially
  • High Ca blocks: Mg and K — common in hard-water grows or over-supplemented calcium
  • High K blocks: Ca and Mg — potassium excess is a frequent source of apparent calcium deficiency
  • High P blocks: Zn and Fe — phosphorus excess causes these deficiency symptoms
  • High Mn blocks: Fe and Mg — manganese toxicity mimics iron and magnesium deficiency
