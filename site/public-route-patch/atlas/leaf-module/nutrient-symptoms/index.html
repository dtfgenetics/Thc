Nutrient Symptoms on Leaves

Leaves can show nutrient clues, but visual symptoms alone are not enough. Nutrient interpretation must include pH, EC, medium, irrigation, plant stage, and root health.

PreviousLeaf ModuleNext: Pest Damage

Location matters

  • Older leaves often show mobile-element issues first.
  • New growth often shows immobile-element or transport-related issues.
  • Tip burn can indicate excess or root-zone concentration.
  • Interveinal patterns narrow the search but do not prove the cause.

What to check

Check feed history, source water, pH, EC/PPM, medium, runoff/root-zone readings when appropriate, dryback, root condition, plant stage, and recent environmental stress.

Common look-alikes

True deficiency, pH-driven availability, salt buildup, overfeeding, underfeeding, root damage, overwatering, drought stress, high VPD, pest feeding, and normal senescence can overlap.
