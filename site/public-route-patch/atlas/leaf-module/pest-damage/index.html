Pest Damage on Leaves

Pest damage often looks like nutrient or environmental stress until the underside, nodes, growing tips, and media surface are inspected.

PreviousLeaf ModuleNext: Environmental Stress

Visual reference slot

Needed asset: Leaf Pest Inspection Card comparing stippling, webbing, eggs, frass, trails, silvering, chewing, and distorted tips.

Field checklist

  • Inspect leaf tops, undersides, veins, petioles, nodes, and growing tips.
  • Look for insects, eggs, webbing, frass, trails, and cast skins.
  • Use magnification and repeat inspection over multiple days.

Measurements to pair

  • Canopy density, humidity, airflow, sanitation notes.
  • Location of first symptoms and spread pattern.
  • Quarantine, introduction, or outdoor exposure history.

Common mistakes

  • Spraying before identifying the pest.
  • Only inspecting leaf tops.
  • Calling stippling a deficiency.

Grow Doc prompt

Inspect for pest damage using tops, undersides, veins, nodes, tips, media surface, magnification, symptom pattern, spread, airflow, humidity, and sanitation history.
