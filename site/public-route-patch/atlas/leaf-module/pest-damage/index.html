Pest Damage on Leaves

Pest feeding on leaf tissue produces distinctive patterns that differ from nutrient, environmental, and pathogen damage. Accurate identification requires a hand lens, knowledge of feeding mechanics, and inspection of the correct surfaces.

← Nutrient SymptomsModule IndexEnvironmental Stress →
Core rule

Always inspect the leaf underside with a hand lens before concluding non-pest cause.

Spider mites, russet mites, thrips, and aphids concentrate on the lower surface. A symptom that looks nutritional, environmental, or fungal from above may have obvious pest evidence visible in 30 seconds with a 10–60× loupe on the underside.

Observation timing

Early detection requires looking before symptoms are severe.

Russet mites and broad mites cause symptoms weeks after populations are established. Spider mites are visible earlier but stippling is often mistaken for spray residue. Scout at least weekly — inspect new growth, mid-canopy, and leaf undersides, not just fan leaves.

Scouting protocol

Systematic inspection

What to check and in what order.

  1. Check yellow sticky traps weekly — adult flying pest activity indicator
  2. Inspect new growth at the growing tip with 60× loupe — broad and russet mites concentrate here
  3. Flip 5–10 mid-canopy fan leaves and inspect undersides — spider mites, thrips larvae, aphid colonies
  4. Check stem joints and petiole bases — aphid and thrip hiding spots
  5. Inspect the lower 20% of the plant for russet mite progression up from the base
  6. Note which plants and which areas of the room show early signs — pests spread directionally from entry points
Do not confuse with

Non-pest symptoms that look similar.

  • Broad mite distortion vs calcium/boron deficiency: use a 100× loupe or send to a lab — treatment is completely different
  • Russet mite silvering vs spray residue: spray residue is uniform; mite damage concentrates at the base and progresses upward
  • Spider mite stippling vs calcium or pH-related spotting: stippling has uniform dot size; check underside for moving insects
  • Thrip streaking vs mechanical damage: thrip damage is always on the upper surface with visible frass
