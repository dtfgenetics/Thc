Pest Damage on Leaves

Pest damage often looks like nutrient or environmental stress until the underside, nodes, growing tips, and media surface are inspected.

Visual inspection guide

Use the three-zone scouting diagram below to inspect undersides, new growth, and stem junctions systematically. Compare stippling, webbing, silvering, feeding marks, and distorted tips, then seek direct evidence such as organisms, eggs, cast skins, or frass.

Interpretation limit: Damage patterns overlap with environmental and spray injury. A schematic or symptom photo does not identify a pest species.

Field checklist

  • Inspect leaf tops, undersides, veins, petioles, nodes, and growing tips.
  • Look for insects, eggs, webbing, frass, trails, and cast skins.
  • Use magnification and repeat inspection over multiple days.

Measurements to pair

  • Canopy density, humidity, airflow, sanitation notes.
  • Location of first symptoms and spread pattern.
  • Quarantine, introduction, or outdoor exposure history.

Common mistakes

  • Spraying before identifying the pest.
  • Only inspecting leaf tops.
  • Calling stippling a deficiency.

Inspect the pattern before identifying a pest

Stippling, silvering, webbing, distorted new growth, and feeding scars overlap with abiotic damage. Confirm with direct observation of organisms, eggs, cast skins, or characteristic feeding evidence and document uncertainty.

Stippling and webbing

Examine both sides of older leaves; note fine feeding specks, moving organisms, eggs, and silk-like strands. Similar dots can result from non-pest damage.

Silver streaks or specks

Check leaf surfaces for feeding scars and dark frass-like deposits; do not identify thrips by silvering alone.

Distorted young leaves

Inspect emerging tissue for tiny pests while also evaluating environmental stress, nutrient transport and spray injury.

Document and verify

  1. Photograph the damage, leaf underside, and whole-plant distribution with a scale reference.
  2. Use suitable magnification to inspect organisms or feeding signs; repeat if results are inconclusive.
  3. Compare affected plants with healthy neighbors and record recent introductions, sprays and irrigation events.
  4. Before choosing controls, verify the likely pest and follow applicable product labels and local rules.

Uncertainty: No symptom image alone establishes pest identity. Not finding a pest during one inspection does not exclude an infestation.
