Stomata

Stomata are adjustable pores that simultaneously regulate CO₂ entry, water vapour loss, and leaf temperature. Their opening state connects VPD, transpiration, calcium transport, and nutrient uptake into one integrated system.

← PhotosynthesisModule IndexModule IndexTranspiration →
Core rule

Stomata modulate continuously — not simply open or closed.

Guard cells respond to light, CO₂, abscisic acid, temperature, and humidity simultaneously. Their aperture integrates all signals at once.

VPD connection

VPD acts on the plant through stomatal aperture.

High VPD increases transpiration demand. When guard cells cannot maintain turgor, they close — reducing CO₂ entry and blocking calcium delivery regardless of root-zone supply.

Guard cell mechanics

opens

Blue light

Activates H⁺-ATPase pumps, driving K⁺ ion influx and increasing guard cell turgor. Rapid response at lights-on.

opens

Low CO₂

When CO₂ drops due to photosynthetic demand, guard cells open wider to allow more CO₂ entry.

closes

Abscisic acid (ABA)

Released under water deficit. Triggers K⁺ efflux from guard cells, reducing turgor and closing the pore. Can occur before wilting is visible.

closes

High VPD

Evaporative demand exceeds water supply through xylem. Guard cells lose turgor and close — even at adequate substrate moisture if VPD spikes rapidly.

consequence

Closed stomata → slow photosynthesis

CO₂ entry blocked regardless of light intensity. Chronic stomatal stress reduces dry weight even when all nutrients are correct.

consequence

Closed stomata → calcium failure

Calcium moves into leaves through the transpiration stream. Low aperture = low transpiration = calcium deficiency at new growth even when substrate calcium is adequate.
