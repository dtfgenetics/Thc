Stomata

Stomata are adjustable pores that simultaneously regulate CO₂ entry, water vapour loss, and leaf temperature. Their changing aperture influences CO₂ diffusion and water loss, while root water supply and environmental conditions also affect transpiration.

← PhotosynthesisModule IndexModule IndexTranspiration →
Core rule

Stomata modulate continuously — not simply open or closed.

Guard cells respond to light, CO₂, abscisic acid, temperature, and humidity simultaneously. Their aperture integrates all signals at once.

VPD connection

VPD and stomatal conductance interact with plant water status.

Higher VPD can increase evaporative demand; stomatal responses depend on plant water status and signaling. Reduced transpiration can change calcium movement, but does not prove a calcium deficiency.

Guard cell mechanics

opens

Blue light

Activates H⁺-ATPase pumps, driving K⁺ ion influx and increasing guard cell turgor. Rapid response at lights-on.

opens

Low CO₂

When CO₂ drops due to photosynthetic demand, guard cells open wider to allow more CO₂ entry.

closes

Abscisic acid (ABA)

ABA signaling can increase during water stress and promote ion fluxes that reduce guard-cell turgor. The response may precede visible wilting.

closes

High VPD

High evaporative demand can reduce stomatal conductance, depending on water supply, leaf temperature and signaling.

consequence

Conductance and carbon uptake

Reduced stomatal conductance may limit carbon uptake, though photosynthesis can also be limited by light, temperature and metabolic capacity.

consequence

Conductance and mineral transport

Calcium transport is influenced by water flow and tissue development. Restricted transpiration can contribute to uneven supply, but a deficiency requires further evidence.
