Stomata

Stomata are adjustable pores that simultaneously control CO₂ entry, water vapour loss, and leaf temperature. Their opening state connects VPD, transpiration, calcium transport, and nutrient uptake into one integrated system.

← PhotosynthesisModule IndexTranspiration →
Core rule

Stomata modulate continuously — not simply open or closed.

Guard cells respond to light, CO₂ concentration, abscisic acid, temperature, and humidity simultaneously. Their aperture integrates all signals at once. A stomata-related symptom usually means one of those inputs is out of range.

VPD connection

VPD acts on the plant through stomatal aperture.

High VPD increases transpiration demand. When guard cells cannot maintain turgor against high evaporative demand, they close — reducing CO₂ entry and blocking calcium delivery regardless of substrate supply.

Guard cell mechanics — opening and closing signals

opens

Blue light

Activates H⁺-ATPase pumps in guard cells, driving K⁺ ion influx and increasing turgor. The guard cells swell, bowing open the pore. Rapid response at lights-on.

opens

Low CO₂

When CO₂ inside the leaf drops due to photosynthetic demand, guard cells open wider to allow more CO₂ entry. High CO₂ causes partial closure.

closes

Abscisic acid (ABA)

Released under water deficit. Triggers K⁺ efflux from guard cells, reducing turgor and closing the pore. The primary drought-response mechanism — can occur before wilting is visible.

closes

High VPD

Evaporative demand exceeds water supply through xylem. Guard cells lose turgor and close. Can happen at adequate substrate moisture if VPD spikes rapidly or root uptake is impaired.

consequence

Closed stomata block CO₂

Photosynthesis slows regardless of light intensity. Chronic stomatal stress reduces dry weight accumulation even when all nutrients and light are correct.

consequence

Closed stomata block calcium

Calcium moves into leaves through the transpiration stream. Low aperture = low transpiration = calcium deficiency symptoms at new growth even when root-zone calcium is adequate.

Observation

Stomatal stress signals

What stomatal dysfunction looks like.

  • Tip burn on new growth: high-VPD calcium delivery failure — not substrate deficiency
  • Upward leaf curl (taco): closure response to heat or high VPD
  • Midday wilt that recovers at lights-off: VPD exceeding root water supply rate
  • Silvery sheen on leaf underside: russet mite or thrip damage to guard cells
  • Powdery mildew entry: pathogen enters open stomata — high RH + low VPD combination
What to measure

Instruments for stomatal diagnosis.

  1. Measure VPD — not just RH. Stomata respond to the vapour pressure gradient, not absolute humidity
  2. Measure leaf temperature — 2–3°C below air is normal; larger difference indicates stomatal or transpiration stress
  3. Check PPFD at the canopy — insufficient light keeps stomata more closed, reducing growth rate
  4. Note time-of-day pattern — stomatal stress symptoms worsen during the high-VPD midday window
