Leaves integrate light capture, photosynthesis, gas exchange, transpiration, vascular supply, and visible stress. A leaf symptom is evidence, not proof: interpret it with tissue age, canopy position, environment, root-zone data, recent changes, and direct pest or pathogen inspection.

Core rule

Observation first. Diagnosis second.

Yellowing, browning, spots, curling, wilting, burnt tips, or pest marks must be described before assigning a cause. Start with old versus new tissue, upper versus lower canopy, symptom distribution, progression, and recent environmental or management changes.

Connected evidence

Leaves are not isolated organs.

Leaf appearance reflects root supply, vascular transport, light, temperature, humidity, gas exchange, mineral status, pests, pathogens, and developmental stage.

Diagnostic principle

Do not label a deficiency from a single leaf photograph. Combine tissue age, symptom distribution, environment, root-zone measurements, pest inspection, and progression over time. When evidence is mixed, continue into Observation & Differential Diagnostics.
