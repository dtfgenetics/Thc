Physiology

Light, temperature, humidity, VPD, CO₂, and airflow are not independent dials — they interact through plant physiology. Adjusting one without understanding how it affects the others is a reliable way to create new problems while trying to fix existing ones.

Core rule

Every environmental parameter affects plant physiology through the same organ — the leaf.

Stomata open and close in response to light, CO₂, humidity, and temperature simultaneously. Changes to any one factor shift the others' effects. VPD sets transpiration demand; light intensity sets photosynthesis rate; temperature sets enzyme activity. Adjust one, measure all.

First measurement principle

Measure at canopy level, not at the sensor.

Air temperature at the sensor, leaf temperature at the canopy, and root zone temperature are three different numbers. Calibrate your readings to the plant's actual experience — not the room average. Leaf-to-air temperature offset of −1 to −3°C is normal in a healthy transpiring plant.

Six key parameters

light

PPFD & Daily Light Integral

PPFD (μmol/m²/s) is instantaneous intensity; DLI (mol/m²/day) is the total daily light dose. Both matter — a high-PPFD short photoperiod may deliver less DLI than a lower PPFD with a longer day.

→ PPFD Calculator
temperature

Temperature & Enzyme Activity

Photosynthesis, respiration, and nutrient uptake are all enzyme-driven with temperature optima. Canopy temperature — not air temperature — is the correct measurement site.

VPD

Humidity & Vapour Pressure Deficit

Relative humidity does not drive plant transpiration — VPD does. High VPD forces high transpiration and calcium transport; low VPD restricts both. Calcium transport is transpiration-dependent.

→ VPD Chart
CO₂

CO₂ & Photosynthetic Ceiling

Supplemental CO₂ raises the photosynthetic ceiling — but only if light intensity and temperature are high enough to drive additional photosynthesis. CO₂ without adequate PPFD produces almost no response.

airflow

Airflow & Boundary Layer

Airflow removes the boundary layer of humid, CO₂-depleted air around each leaf and provides mechanical stimulus that strengthens stems (thigmomorphogenesis).

root zone

Root Zone Temperature

Root enzyme activity, water uptake rate, and pathogen susceptibility are all temperature-dependent. Below 18°C slows uptake; above 28°C promotes Pythium. Root zone diverging from air temp by more than ~8°C creates uptake stress.

Environmental symptom map

Interaction effects

What changes together.

  • Temperature ↑ + RH constant → VPD ↑: raising temperature without increasing RH always raises VPD and transpiration demand
  • High VPD → calcium deficiency symptoms: transpiration drives calcium — low VPD = low transport to new growth
  • Low CO₂ + high light: photosynthesis is CO₂-limited before light-limited at normal atmospheric levels above ~600 μmol/m²/s
  • Root zone cold + foliar correct: plant shows stress symptoms despite correct above-ground environment
Symptom map

Environmental vs nutritional.

  • Tip burn (new growth): high VPD or low airflow → calcium transport failure
  • Bleaching / light burn: PPFD above plant tolerance at current temperature and CO₂ level
  • Leaf curl up (taco): high temperature, high VPD, or heat stress
  • Leaf curl down: overwatering, root problems, or high humidity with low VPD
  • Sudden mid-day wilt: high VPD spike, root zone temperature excess, or root damage
  • Botrytis in dense buds: high humidity + inadequate airflow through canopy
