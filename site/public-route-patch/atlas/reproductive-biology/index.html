Core concepts

What belongs in this system

Sex expressionPollenAntherStigmaPollinationFertilizationEmbryoSeed maturation
Function

What the system does

  • Produces male and female gametophytes
  • Transfers genetic material through pollen
  • Combines parental genomes at fertilization
  • Builds the next generation inside the developing seed
Observation

What to record

  • Location and timing of reproductive structures
  • Pollen release
  • Post-pollination changes
  • Seed swelling and maturation
Interactions

Keep connected causes in view

  • Genotype influences sex expression
  • Photoperiod and developmental signals regulate flowering
  • Stress can alter reproductive development
  • Pollination redirects resources toward seed formation
Measurements

Attach numbers to the observation

  • Developmental stage
  • Photoperiod history
  • Location of reproductive structures
  • Pollen presence
  • Pollination date when known
  • Seed development timing
Evidence questions

Test the explanation

  • Has the structure been anatomically identified before assigning sex expression?
  • Is pollen exposure documented or only suspected?
  • Do reproductive changes align with stage, genotype and environmental history?
Interpretation cautions

Avoid single-symptom conclusions

  • Sex expression is a biological trait with genetic and environmental components
  • One structure should be identified anatomically before conclusions are drawn
  • Pollen movement can occur beyond the immediately visible plant

Evidence boundary: observation narrows plausible explanations; it does not prove a cause by itself. Use measurements, time course, comparisons, and appropriate testing before acting.
