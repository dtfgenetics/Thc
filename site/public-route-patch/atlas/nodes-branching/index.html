Core rule

Apical dominance shapes everything above ground.

The shoot apex actively suppresses axillary buds through auxin signalling. Removing or redirecting the apex — by topping, FIM, or bending — releases that suppression and shifts resources into lateral branches. The plant's response depends on timing, health, and how the intervention is made.

Observation priority

Internodal spacing is an environmental signal

Tight internodes indicate adequate light intensity and spectrum at the canopy. Stretched, long internodes typically indicate insufficient light, high red-to-far-red ratio, excessive heat, or hormonal imbalance. Internode length is a faster environmental feedback signal than leaf colour changes.

Training timing

When to intervene matters as much as how

  • Topping before node 5–6 delays the canopy without enough established root mass
  • Topping after week 2 of flower stresses resin sites without canopy benefit
  • LST can begin earlier and continue into early flower without the same recovery cost
  • SCROG fill targets are set by internodal spacing, not calendar weeks
  • All training increases the plant's stress load — time with feeding, environment, and health status
Observation guide

What to measure at nodes

  1. Count nodes from the base, not the top — establishes consistent position references
  2. Measure internode distance between node 3 and node 5 as a stretch indicator
  3. Check axillary bud development 48–72 hours after topping for response confirmation
  4. Monitor new growth colour: pale new growth at the apex may indicate mobile nutrient stress accelerated by training
  5. Inspect petiole insertion points for pest entry — thrips and broad mites concentrate at new growth
