Root Zone Diagnostics

Diagnosing root zone problems from plant symptoms requires systematic measurement — pH, EC, dissolved oxygen, temperature, moisture, and root inspection — in the right sequence.

← Root RotModule Index
Core rule

Diagnose the root zone before treating the canopy.

The vast majority of foliar symptoms originate in the root zone. A plant showing nutrient deficiency, wilting, or slow growth that does not respond to foliar or nutritional interventions almost certainly has a root zone problem. Check the root zone first — always.

Measurement sequence

Measure in this order: pH, EC, temperature, moisture, then inspect roots.

Each measurement either confirms a root zone cause or rules it out. Starting with root inspection before measuring pH, EC, and temperature misses the most common causes and risks contaminating the root system during inspection.

Root zone diagnostic protocol

Measure runoff pH

Irrigate with 20–30% more volume than usual. Collect runoff. Test pH immediately. Root zone pH drifting from target by more than 0.5 pH units affects multiple nutrients simultaneously.

Measure runoff EC

Compare runoff EC to input EC. Runoff EC higher than input indicates salt accumulation — flush required. Lower than input may indicate excessive dilution or poor substrate buffering.

Measure root zone temperature

Insert thermometer into substrate at mid-depth. Target 18–24°C. Above 26°C enables pythium; below 18°C slows enzyme-driven uptake.

Assess moisture and dryback

Weigh the container at the time of the diagnosis. Compare to known field capacity. Determine whether the plant is in the correct dryback range for its stage.

Oxygen assessment

In hydro: measure dissolved oxygen directly. In substrate: assess pore space by how quickly drainage flows after irrigation — slow drainage indicates compacted or over-saturated substrate.

Root inspection

Only after measuring above — carefully remove plant, inspect root colour, texture, and smell. Confirm or rule out active root rot before determining a treatment plan.
