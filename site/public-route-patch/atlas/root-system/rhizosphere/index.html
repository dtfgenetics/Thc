Rhizosphere

The rhizosphere is the narrow zone of soil directly influenced by root activity. It supports complex microbial communities, pH gradients, and exudate chemistry that determine nutrient availability beyond what bulk substrate pH measures.

Core rule

Root-zone pH at the root surface differs from bulk substrate pH.

Roots acidify the rhizosphere through proton extrusion (H⁺) during cation uptake and alkalinise it during anion uptake. The actual pH experienced by root hairs — where uptake occurs — may differ from the pH measured in runoff by 0.5–1.5 units.

Microbial community

Rhizosphere microbes directly affect nutrient availability.

Mycorrhizal fungi extend effective root surface area 10–100×. Beneficial bacteria solubilise phosphorus, fix atmospheric nitrogen, and suppress pathogen colonisation. Disrupting this community through over-sterilisation or biocidal inputs reduces uptake efficiency.

Rhizosphere components

biology

Mycorrhizal Fungi

Symbiotic fungi that colonise root cortex cells (arbuscular mycorrhizae) or root surfaces (ectomycorrhizae). In exchange for plant sugars, they extend hyphal networks far beyond the root depletion zone — accessing phosphorus, zinc, and copper the root alone cannot reach.

biology

Rhizobacteria

Bacteria that colonise root surfaces and the immediate zone around them. Plant-growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) produce growth hormones, solubilise locked phosphorus, and compete with pathogenic organisms for colonisation sites.

chemistry

Root Exudates

Sugars, amino acids, organic acids, and phenolic compounds secreted by roots. Attract beneficial microbes, acidify the rhizosphere to mobilise iron and phosphorus, and serve as root-to-root and root-to-microbe chemical signals.

pH

Rhizosphere pH Gradient

The zone closest to the root surface is typically 0.5–1.5 pH units lower than bulk substrate pH due to H⁺ extrusion during cation uptake. Measuring runoff pH gives a bulk average — not the pH at the actual uptake site.

threat

Pathogenic Organisms

Pythium, Fusarium, Rhizoctonia, and Phytophthora are the primary root pathogens in cannabis production. All exploit low-oxygen, high-moisture, or stressed root zones. A healthy rhizosphere microbiome suppresses them through competitive exclusion.

depletion zone

Root Depletion Zone

The area immediately around each root hair where a nutrient has been depleted faster than it diffuses from bulk substrate. How quickly this zone develops determines whether diffusion or mass flow governs uptake for that element.

Managing the rhizosphere

Protective practices

What supports a healthy rhizosphere.

  • Maintain adequate oxygen levels — 8–12 ppm dissolved oxygen in hydro; good aeration in substrates
  • Avoid overwatering — saturated substrate is anaerobic and suppresses beneficial microbes
  • Inoculate with verified mycorrhizal and PGPR products at transplant — not during nutrient-heavy phases
  • Maintain EC within range — very high EC suppresses microbial diversity and stresses root hairs
What disrupts it

Inputs that damage rhizosphere biology.

  • Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂): sterilises root zone — kills beneficial microbes alongside pathogens
  • Chlorinated water: suppresses mycorrhizae — allow to off-gas or use carbon filtration
  • High salt EC: creates osmotic stress that damages root hair membranes and microbial cells
  • Rapid pH swings: destabilises microbial community equilibrium and causes nutrient availability oscillation
