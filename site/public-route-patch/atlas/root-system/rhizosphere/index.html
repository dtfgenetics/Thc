Rhizosphere

The rhizosphere is the narrow zone directly influenced by root activity. It supports microbial communities, pH gradients, and exudate chemistry that determine nutrient availability beyond what bulk substrate pH measures.

← Root AnatomyModule IndexModule IndexWater Uptake →
Core rule

Root-zone pH at the root surface differs from bulk substrate pH.

Roots acidify the rhizosphere through proton extrusion during cation uptake. The actual pH at root hairs — where uptake occurs — may differ from runoff pH by 0.5–1.5 units.

Microbial community

Rhizosphere microbes directly affect nutrient availability and pathogen suppression.

Mycorrhizal fungi extend effective root surface area 10–100×. Beneficial bacteria solubilise phosphorus, fix nitrogen, and suppress pathogen colonisation. Disrupting this community reduces uptake efficiency.

Rhizosphere components

biology

Mycorrhizal Fungi

Symbiotic fungi extending hyphal networks far beyond the root depletion zone — accessing phosphorus, zinc, and copper the root alone cannot reach. Exchange plant sugars for mineral delivery.

biology

Rhizobacteria

Plant-growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) produce growth hormones, solubilise locked phosphorus, and compete with pathogens for colonisation sites on root surfaces.

chemistry

Root Exudates

Sugars, amino acids, organic acids, and phenolic compounds secreted by roots. Attract beneficial microbes, acidify the rhizosphere to mobilise iron and phosphorus, and serve as chemical signals.

pH

Rhizosphere pH Gradient

Typically 0.5–1.5 pH units lower than bulk substrate due to H⁺ extrusion during cation uptake. Measuring runoff pH gives a bulk average — not the pH at the actual uptake site.

threat

Pathogenic Organisms

Pythium, Fusarium, and Phytophthora exploit low-oxygen, high-moisture, or stressed root zones. A healthy rhizosphere microbiome suppresses them through competitive exclusion.

depletion

Root Depletion Zone

The area immediately around each root hair where a nutrient has been depleted faster than it diffuses from bulk substrate. How quickly this develops determines whether diffusion or mass flow governs uptake.
