Water Uptake

Water enters roots by osmosis and moves upward through xylem driven by the transpiration stream. When substrate EC is very high, the gradient reverses — water exits roots into the substrate, causing osmotic stress.

← RhizosphereModule IndexModule IndexNutrient Uptake →
Core rule

Water moves into roots by osmosis — from lower to higher solute concentration.

Root cells maintain higher solute concentration than surrounding soil water. This drives passive water entry. Very high substrate EC reverses this gradient — water moves out of roots, causing osmotic stress even when moisture is adequate.

Three pathways

Water can move through, around, or between root cells.

Apoplastic (through cell walls), symplastic (through cytoplasm), and transmembrane (through aquaporin channels). Aquaporins are the regulated channel — their expression responds to drought, temperature, and nutrient status.
