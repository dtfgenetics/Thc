Nutrient Uptake

Nutrient uptake is regulated at the root — pH, oxygen, root health, ion antagonism, and plant demand all determine what the plant can actually access regardless of what is in the substrate.

← Water UptakeModule IndexModule IndexRoot Oxygen →
Core rule

The correct nutrient at the wrong pH is unavailable.

pH-dependent solubility and charge determine what the plant can absorb. Iron precipitates above pH 6.5 in most substrates. Checking pH before adding nutrients is not optional — it determines whether the addition has any effect.

Active uptake

Most nutrient uptake is active — energy-dependent and oxygen-sensitive.

The plant uses ATP to pump specific ions across root membranes. This process is temperature-sensitive and oxygen-dependent. A root under oxygen stress cannot run active transport — creating deficiency symptoms even when substrate levels are adequate.
