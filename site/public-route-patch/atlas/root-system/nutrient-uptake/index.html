Nutrient Uptake

Nutrient uptake is regulated at the root — not simply determined by what is in the substrate. pH, oxygen, root health, antagonism between ions, and plant demand all determine what the plant can actually access.

← Water UptakeModule IndexRoot Oxygen →
Core rule

The correct nutrient at the wrong pH is unavailable.

Nutrient availability is determined by pH-dependent solubility and charge. Iron precipitates above pH 6.5 in most substrates. Calcium becomes less available below pH 6.0. Checking pH before adding nutrients is not optional — it determines whether the addition has any effect at all.

Active vs passive

Most nutrient uptake is active — energy-dependent and selective.

The plant uses ATP to pump specific ions across root membranes against concentration gradients. This process is temperature-sensitive, oxygen-dependent, and regulated by plant demand. A root under oxygen stress cannot run active transport — creating nutrient deficiency symptoms even when substrate levels are adequate.

Uptake mechanisms and what disrupts them

Ion competition

How ions compete at the root membrane.

  • Excess ammonium (NH₄⁺): competes with Ca²⁺ and K⁺ at cation channels — classic calcium and potassium deficiency under high-ammonium feeds
  • Excess potassium: blocks Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ — very common cause of apparent calcium deficiency
  • Excess calcium: competes with Mg²⁺ and K⁺ — causes magnesium deficiency
  • Excess phosphorus: precipitates Zn²⁺ and Fe³⁺ in the substrate — deficiency symptoms appear
What stops uptake

Root-zone conditions that block nutrient transport.

  • Oxygen deficit: active ion transport requires ATP — anaerobic roots cannot run it. Overwatering kills uptake before roots visibly rot
  • Cold substrate: enzyme activity and membrane transport slow sharply below 18°C
  • High EC: osmotic stress limits water entry — nutrients cannot enter efficiently in a water-deficit state
  • Root damage: pythium, fusarium, or physical damage reduce absorptive surface area regardless of substrate content
