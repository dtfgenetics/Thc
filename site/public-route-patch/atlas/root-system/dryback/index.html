Dryback

Dryback is the controlled drying cycle between irrigations. Correct dryback simultaneously delivers oxygen to roots and drives calcium transport to leaves — two completely different physiological functions in one management decision.

← Root OxygenModule IndexModule IndexRoot Rot →
Core rule

Dryback delivers oxygen to roots and drives calcium transport to leaves simultaneously.

As substrate dries, air re-enters the pore space previously filled with water — primary root zone aeration mechanism. Simultaneously, moderate dryback maintains mild water deficit that keeps stomata open and calcium delivery active.

Container weight

Weight is the most reliable dryback indicator.

Weigh the container at field capacity (fully watered and drained) and at a dry reference. The percentage of weight lost between irrigations directly measures dryback. Visual inspection and finger-testing are unreliable — they assess the surface, not the root zone.
