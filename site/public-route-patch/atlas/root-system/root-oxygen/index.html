Root Oxygen

Root respiration requires oxygen. Anaerobic root zones kill root hair function and enable the pathogens that cause root rot — often long before wilting or visual symptoms appear at the canopy.

← Nutrient UptakeModule IndexDryback →
Core rule

Roots respire aerobically — oxygen deficit stops uptake before roots die.

Root cells use oxygen to produce ATP through aerobic respiration. Even brief periods of oxygen deprivation reduce active ion transport and root growth. Persistent oxygen deprivation progresses to root hair death, then to conditions enabling pythium and other anaerobic pathogens.

Overwatering mechanism

Overwatering kills roots through oxygen exclusion, not water itself.

When substrate pores are saturated, gas exchange stops. CO₂ accumulates and O₂ is depleted in the root zone within hours. Roots shift to anaerobic respiration, producing ethanol — toxic to root cells. Pythium spores, which require water for motility, then colonise the stressed root tissue.

Oxygen in different growing systems

soil / coco

Pore Space Aeration

In substrates, oxygen is delivered through air-filled pores. Field capacity (just after drainage) typically leaves 20–30% air-filled pore space. As substrate dries, air-filled porosity increases. Overwatering collapses this space entirely.

hydro

Dissolved Oxygen (DO)

In hydroponic systems, oxygen dissolves in the nutrient solution. Target 8–12 ppm DO at the root zone. DO decreases as temperature rises — a reservoir at 26°C holds significantly less oxygen than one at 18°C.

threat

Pythium & Anaerobic Pathogens

Pythium produces motile zoospores that swim toward stressed root tissue in saturated conditions. Once established, it progresses rapidly — particularly in warm, low-oxygen root zones. Phytophthora follows the same pattern.

coco

Coco Coir Oxygen Dynamics

Coco retains more water than most substrates but also has excellent air-filled porosity when managed with correct dry-back cycling. Its high water-holding capacity makes overwatering easy — dryback discipline is essential.

Oxygen and dryback management

Target conditions

How to maintain adequate root oxygen.

  • Allow substrate to reach 10–20% dryback from field capacity between irrigations in coco/soil systems
  • Check container weight — a fully watered container vs a field-capacity container are distinct
  • Hydro: maintain dissolved oxygen above 8 ppm; keep reservoir below 22°C
  • Use airstones or recirculating systems to maintain DO in reservoirs
  • Ensure containers have adequate drainage holes — standing water at the base creates anaerobic zone
Oxygen deficit signs

Early indicators before visual root damage.

  • Slow uptake despite correct feed: drooping plants with correct substrate moisture — roots not delivering
  • Yellowing despite correct pH and EC: nutrient uptake failure from anaerobic root zone
  • Container stays heavy longer than expected: substrate not draining or drying — oxygen starved zone developing
  • Sour or rotten smell from substrate or drainage: anaerobic decomposition underway
  • Slow root mass expansion: roots not extending into fresh substrate — oxygen or EC block
