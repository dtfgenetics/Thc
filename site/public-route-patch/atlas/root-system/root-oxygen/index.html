Root Oxygen

Root respiration requires oxygen. Anaerobic root zones kill root hair function and enable pythium — often long before any visible wilting or canopy symptoms appear.

← Nutrient UptakeModule IndexModule IndexDryback →
Core rule

Roots respire aerobically — oxygen deficit stops uptake before roots die.

Root cells use oxygen to produce ATP. Even brief oxygen deprivation reduces active ion transport and root growth. Persistent deprivation progresses to root hair death, then to conditions enabling pythium and other anaerobic pathogens.

Overwatering mechanism

Overwatering kills roots through oxygen exclusion — not water itself.

When substrate pores are saturated, gas exchange stops. CO₂ accumulates and O₂ depletes within hours. Roots shift to anaerobic respiration, producing ethanol — toxic to root cells. Pythium spores then colonise stressed tissue.

Root oxygen, hypoxia, and recovery

Root-zone oxygen pathway: Persistent saturation → less air-filled pore space → reduced oxygen availability → impaired root respiration. Evaluate drainage, irrigation timing, and root health before diagnosing a pathogen.

Oxygen deficit signs

Early indicators

Before visible root damage.

  • Slow uptake despite correct feed: drooping plants with correct substrate moisture
  • Yellowing despite correct pH and EC: nutrient uptake failure from anaerobic zone
  • Container stays heavy longer than expected: oxygen-starved zone developing
  • Sour or rotten smell from substrate: anaerobic decomposition underway
Prevention

Maintaining adequate root oxygen.

  • Allow 10–20% dryback from field capacity between irrigations in coco/soil
  • Check container weight — a fully watered vs field-capacity container are distinct
  • Hydro: maintain dissolved oxygen above 8 ppm; keep reservoir below 22°C
  • Ensure containers have adequate drainage holes — standing water creates anaerobic zone
