Root Anatomy

Root architecture determines uptake capacity, anchorage, and water distribution. Understanding which zones do what makes root zone management decisions more accurate and root health assessment more reliable.

Core rule

Root health is the first check for any plant symptom.

Most foliar symptoms originate in the root zone. A root system under oxygen stress, pathogen attack, or pH imbalance fails to deliver minerals and water — producing symptoms often misread as nutritional at the leaf level.

30-second check

Healthy vs damaged roots in one inspection.

Healthy: white, firm, faintly earthy smell. Damaged: brown, grey, or black; soft or slimy; rotten or fishy smell. Root inspection should precede any nutritional or environmental intervention.

Root zones

structure

Taproot

The first root to emerge from a germinating seed. Sets the vertical axis of root architecture. In containers, reaches the base and branches extensively.

structure

Lateral Roots

Branch from the main root axis. Increase total surface area and access different substrate zones. Dense lateral development indicates healthy growing conditions.

uptake

Root Hair Zone

Fine single-cell extensions that dramatically increase absorptive surface area. Fragile — easily destroyed by physical disturbance, salt stress, or pathogen pressure. Primary site of water and mineral uptake.

growth

Root Meristem

Actively dividing zone at the root tip. New cells produced here push the tip into fresh substrate. Damage halts root extension in that branch.

protection

Root Cap

Protective mass of cells covering the root meristem. Secretes mucilage that lubricates penetration through substrate. Continuously replaced as outer cells shed.

transport

Vascular Cylinder (Stele)

Central core of xylem and phloem. The Casparian strip controls what enters. Xylem loaded here carries water and minerals to the shoot.

Root inspection guide

Healthy root indicators

What good roots look like.

  • Colour: white to off-white; light cream in buffered substrates
  • Texture: firm, slightly flexible, not slimy
  • Smell: faintly earthy — not offensive
  • Coverage: dense, fibrous root tips throughout the substrate mass
Damaged root indicators

What to look for when roots fail.

  • Brown or grey roots: oxygen deprivation, pythium, or salt stress
  • Black or slimy roots: advanced pythium or fusarium — often fishy smell
  • Sparse root development: overwatered substrate, compacted medium, or high EC
