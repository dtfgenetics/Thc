Core rule

The flower is a diagnostic site, not just a harvest indicator.

Bract swelling, stigma colour, trichome density, and resin clarity each signal different biological stages. Reading them together — not separately — gives a more accurate picture of plant maturity and stress history.

Sex observation priority

Distinguish sex expression early, and consistently.

Pistillate and staminate structures appear at different times and locations. Pre-flower pistils at nodes appear before photoperiod-triggered flowering. Hermaphrodite tendencies and stress-induced staminate development require early and consistent inspection throughout the run.

Sex identification & inspection

Key distinctions

Pistillate vs staminate vs hermaphrodite.

  • Female pre-flowers: two white hairs (pistils) at nodes, no ball structures
  • Male pre-flowers: round ball structures at nodes, no pistils
  • Nanners: banana-shaped staminate structures inside pistillate flowers, stress-induced — a single overlooked nanner can pollinate a whole room
  • True hermaphrodite: both sac-type male and pistillate flowers on same plant
Inspection protocol

Flower inspection sequence.

  1. Confirm plant sex before flowering is induced
  2. Check early pre-flower nodes on week 1–2 of flower for pistils only
  3. Inspect every 3–4 days for nanner development mid-flower
  4. Begin trichome inspection at week 5–6 with a jeweller's loupe or scope
  5. Integrate stigma colour, bract swelling, and whole-plant signals at end of run
  6. Sample consistently: same position on the plant, same time of day
