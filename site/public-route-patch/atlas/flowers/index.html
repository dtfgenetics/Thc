Reproduction Deep Lab · Flower Anatomy

Flower anatomy, from visible structure to reproductive function.

Connect bracts, ovaries, stigmas, anthers, inflorescence architecture, sugar leaves, and trichome-bearing surfaces to reproductive development. Anatomy can support observation, but it does not by itself establish potency, chemistry, cultivar identity, or harvest readiness.

Staminate anatomy

Anthers + pollen structures

Microclimate

Dense flowers create local environments

Flower-zone temperature, RH, airflow, tissue density, and wetness can differ from room averages. Record local context when evaluating stress or mold risk.

Maturity

Do not use one visible feature alone

Stigma color is not a complete maturity metric. Pair developmental timing and tissue observations with appropriate microscopy and measured context.

Evidence boundary: flower appearance does not directly measure cannabinoids or terpenes.
