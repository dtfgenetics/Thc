Observation framework

What to record before making a conclusion

  • Plant developmental stage and days since photoperiod transition, when known.
  • Which flower position is being observed: top, middle, lower canopy, or lateral site.
  • Bract expansion, stigma condition, visible seed formation, tissue damage, pests, or abnormal growth.
  • Representative photographs or microscopy images with scale and lighting kept consistent.
  • Environmental and irrigation context when the question involves stress or abnormal development.

Evidence boundary: visible flower anatomy can support observation and comparison, but it does not by itself establish cannabinoid content, terpene content, pathogen identity, genetic identity, or harvest readiness.
