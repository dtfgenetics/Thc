Stress, Defense & Acclimation

Study how plants perceive and respond to heat, cold, drought, salinity, excess light, mechanical injury, herbivory, and pathogens while distinguishing reversible acclimation from damage.

Use the diagnostics workflowBack to all systems
Core concepts

What belongs in this system

AcclimationOxidative stressHeatColdDroughtSalinityWoundingDefense signaling
Function

What the system does

  • Protects cellular systems under changing conditions
  • Reallocates resources during stress
  • Activates structural and chemical defenses
  • Supports recovery when conditions improve
Observation

What to record

  • Timing of symptoms
  • Affected tissue age and position
  • Recovery after conditions change
  • Lesion expansion, pest evidence, and mechanical injury
Interactions

Keep connected causes in view

  • Multiple stresses can occur simultaneously
  • Water status interacts with heat and salinity
  • Defense signaling can reduce growth
  • Genotype affects tolerance and acclimation
Measurements

Attach numbers to the observation

  • Stress onset time
  • Maximum/minimum environmental excursion
  • Affected tissue position
  • Progression rate
  • Recovery after correction
  • Pest/pathogen evidence
  • Recent mechanical injury
Evidence questions

Test the explanation

  • Is the response reversible acclimation or irreversible damage?
  • Are several stresses occurring simultaneously?
  • Does symptom timing match the suspected stress event?
Interpretation cautions

Avoid single-symptom conclusions

  • Stress symptoms often overlap
  • Damage and acclimation are not the same
  • Reconstruct recent environmental and management changes before ranking causes

Evidence boundary: observation narrows plausible explanations; it does not prove a cause by itself. Use measurements, time course, comparisons, and appropriate testing before acting.
