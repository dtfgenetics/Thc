Trichomes are the plant's secretory organs, not decorative features. Understanding their types, distribution, development, and maturation state is the foundation of accurate harvest timing and resin quality assessment.

Core rule

Trichome maturation is not linear and not uniform across the plant.

Apical buds mature before lower bud sites. Calyxes mature before sugar leaves. Sampling consistently from the same position — ideally a mid-canopy calyx bract — is the only way to track the actual maturation arc rather than averaging across incompatible sites.

Observation standard

Head clarity is a biosynthesis proxy, not a direct cannabinoid reading.

Clear heads contain early precursors. Cloudy heads indicate peak biosynthesis. Amber heads reflect oxidation and degradation. A combination of cloudy with some amber is the most common targeted harvest window — the ratio depends on the chemistry profile sought.

Trichome types

Know what you are examining before reading what it shows.

glandular

Capitate-Stalked

The largest and most resin-dense type. A multicellular stalk topped by a basal disc cell and a large secretory head. Primary site of cannabinoid and terpene biosynthesis. Visible to the naked eye on dense floral tissue.

glandular

Capitate-Sessile

Smaller than stalked trichomes, head sitting directly on the epidermis without an elevated stalk. Numerous across all aerial surfaces. Also biosynthetically active but with lower secretory capacity per head.

glandular

Bulbous

The smallest glandular type — one to two cells, found across all above-ground surfaces. Visible only at high magnification. Biosynthetically active but small secretory volume. More relevant to surface chemistry distribution than harvest timing.

non-glandular

Cystolithic Hair

Non-secretory hair-like structures responsible for the rough texture of fan leaves. Not involved in resin production. Do not indicate maturity or resin content. Should not be confused with secretory trichomes.

maturation

Clear / Translucent Heads

Biosynthesis is underway but precursors have not fully converted. Harvest at this stage preserves more terpene precursors but captures fewer end-stage cannabinoids.

maturation

Cloudy / Milky Heads

Head has become opaque white — peak biosynthetic activity and maximum cannabinoid/terpene content under current environmental conditions. Most common targeted harvest window.

maturation

Amber Heads

Head has oxidised and certain volatile compounds have degraded or converted. Amber proportion increases with post-peak time. Total terpene content is lower. Some growers target specific ratios for specific effect profiles.

maturation

Collapsed / Dark Heads

Head has fully oxidised and collapsed. Indicates overripeness — significant degradation of primary compounds has occurred. Not a target harvest state for any profile.

Consistent sampling protocol

Sampling method

How to read maturation accurately.

  1. Select the same position each inspection — a mid-canopy calyx from a primary cola
  2. Sample at the same time of day — terpene content fluctuates with heat and light cycle
  3. Use a minimum 60× jeweller's loupe; 100× for reliable head clarity assessment
  4. Examine 10–15 individual heads per sample, not a single field of view
  5. Record with date, position, and ratio of clear/cloudy/amber
  6. Integrate with stigma colour change, bract swelling, and aroma development
Four maturation signals

Use all four together, not just one.

  • Trichome head state: clear → cloudy → amber arc across capitate-stalked heads on calyxes
  • Stigma colour: white pistils recede and turn orange/red as the plant matures. 70–90% colour change often coincides with peak trichome development
  • Bract swelling: calyx tissue swells as resin glands enlarge. Dense, swollen calyxes indicate mature resin development
  • Whole-plant senescence: fan leaf yellowing, reduced water uptake, natural leaf drop signal the plant completing its reproductive cycle
