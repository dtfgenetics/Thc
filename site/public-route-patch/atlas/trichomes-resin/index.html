Trichomes & Resin

Trichomes are the plant's secretory organs — not decorative features. Understanding their types, distribution, development, and maturation is the foundation of accurate harvest timing and resin quality assessment.

Core rule

Trichome maturation is not linear and not uniform across the plant.

Apical buds mature before lower bud sites. Calyxes mature before sugar leaves. Sampling consistently from the same position — ideally a mid-canopy calyx bract — is the only way to track the actual maturation arc rather than averaging across incompatible sites.

Observation standard

Trichome head clarity is a biosynthesis proxy, not a direct cannabinoid reading

Clear heads contain early precursors. Cloudy heads indicate peak biosynthesis. Amber heads reflect oxidation and degradation of volatile components. A combination of cloudy with some amber is the most common targeted harvest window — but the ratio depends on the chemistry profile sought.

Trichome types

Know what you are looking at

glandular

Capitate-Stalked Trichomes

The largest and most resin-dense type. A multicellular stalk topped by a basal disc cell and a large secretory head. The primary site of cannabinoid and terpene biosynthesis. Visible to the naked eye on dense floral tissue; best examined at 60–100×.

glandular

Capitate-Sessile Trichomes

Smaller than stalked trichomes, with a head sitting directly on the epidermis without an elevated stalk. Numerous across all aerial surfaces including fan leaves. Also biosynthetically active but with lower secretory capacity per head than stalked types.

glandular

Bulbous Trichomes

The smallest glandular type, one to two cells, found across all above-ground surfaces. Visible only at high magnification. Biosynthetically active but small secretory volume per unit. More relevant to understanding surface chemistry distribution than harvest timing.

non-glandular

Cystolithic (Non-Glandular) Trichomes

Non-secretory hair-like structures on leaf surfaces — responsible for the rough texture of fan leaves. Not involved in resin production and should not be confused with secretory trichomes. Do not indicate maturity or resin content.

Maturation tracking

Head state and what it indicates

early

Clear / Translucent Heads

Trichome head is transparent under magnification. Indicates biosynthesis is underway but precursors have not fully converted. Harvest at this stage preserves more terpene precursors but captures fewer end-stage cannabinoids.

peak

Cloudy / Milky White Heads

Head has become opaque white. Indicates peak biosynthetic activity and maximum cannabinoid/terpene content under the specific environmental conditions. Most growers targeting psychoactive profiles aim for primarily cloudy with some amber transition.

mature

Amber Heads

Head has oxidised and certain volatile compounds have degraded or converted. Amber proportion increases with post-peak time. Higher amber ratios are sometimes sought for specific effect profiles involving degradation products, but total terpene content is lower.

degraded

Collapsed / Dark Heads

Head has fully oxidised and collapsed. Indicates overripeness. At this stage, significant degradation of primary compounds has occurred. Not a target harvest state.

Consistent sampling protocol

  1. Select the same position each inspection — a mid-canopy calyx from a primary cola is standard
  2. Sample at the same time of day — terpene content fluctuates with heat and light cycle
  3. Use a minimum 60× jeweller's loupe, 100× for reliable head clarity assessment
  4. Examine 10–15 individual heads per sample, not a single field of view
  5. Record your observations with date, position, and ratio of clear/cloudy/amber
  6. Integrate trichome data with stigma colour change, bract swelling, and aroma development

Integrated harvest indicators

Trichome head state is one of four key maturation signals. Use all four together:

  • Stigma colour: white pistils recede and turn orange/red as the plant matures. 70–90% colour change often coincides with peak trichome development
  • Bract swelling: calyx tissue swells as resin glands enlarge. Dense, swollen calyxes indicate mature resin development
  • Aroma: terpene expression is strongest at peak biosynthesis — notable aroma change signals active maturation
  • Whole-plant senescence: fan leaf yellowing, reduced water uptake, and natural leaf drop signal the plant is completing its reproductive cycle
