Function

What the system does

  • Provides elements required for proteins, nucleic acids, membranes, pigments, and enzymes
  • Supports osmotic regulation and charge balance
  • Enables energy transfer and metabolism
  • Contributes to structural and signaling processes
Observation

What to record

  • Whether symptoms begin in older or newer tissue
  • Interveinal versus uniform chlorosis
  • Tip, margin, spot, or deformation patterns
  • Root-zone pH, EC, irrigation history, and root condition
Interactions

Keep connected causes in view

  • pH changes chemical availability
  • Ions compete and interact
  • Transpiration affects delivery of some nutrients
  • Root health and microbial activity influence uptake
Measurements

Attach numbers to the observation

  • Input solution pH
  • Input EC
  • Root-zone or runoff pH when meaningful
  • Root-zone or runoff EC when meaningful
  • Feed composition and concentration
  • Irrigation history
  • Symptom location by tissue age
Evidence questions

Test the explanation

  • Is the pattern consistent with element mobility and tissue age?
  • Could pH, salinity, root health or water status be limiting uptake rather than supply?
  • Does changing nutrition explain the trend better than environmental or pathogen alternatives?
Interpretation cautions

Avoid single-symptom conclusions

  • Leaf color alone cannot confirm a deficiency
  • Excess and deficiency can overlap visually
  • Correct diagnosis requires plant pattern plus root-zone and management evidence

Evidence boundary: observation narrows plausible explanations; it does not prove a cause by itself. Use measurements, time course, comparisons, and appropriate testing before acting.
