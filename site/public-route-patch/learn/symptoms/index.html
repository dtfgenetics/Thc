Symptom Reading

Visible plant symptoms are evidence — not diagnoses. This guide connects observable patterns to the biological mechanisms that produce them, and provides a structured path from symptom to cause.

Plant Atlas →DiagnosticsField CardsTools
Core rule

Describe the symptom precisely before naming a cause.

Location, distribution pattern, affected tissue age, progression speed, and measurable context must be established first. A cause list built on precise description is far more accurate than one built on a visual match to a single deficiency chart.

Where to start

Old tissue or new tissue — that is the first question.

Mobile nutrients (N, Mg, K, P) are relocated from old to new growth — deficiency appears on older leaves first. Immobile nutrients (Ca, Fe, Mn, B) cannot be moved — deficiency always appears on new growth. This single observation cuts the differential list in half.

Symptom categories

yellowing

Chlorosis

Yellowing from reduced chlorophyll — the pattern and location tell you far more than the overall colour. Uniform, interveinal, marginal, or tip-localised yellowing each points to a different set of causes.

→ Chlorosis lesson
cell death

Necrosis

Brown or black dead tissue. Margin necrosis, tip burn, spots with halos, and random brown patches are different patterns with different causes — the edge of the necrosis and its location are the diagnostic keys.

→ Necrosis lesson
deformation

Leaf Curl & Deformation

Upward curl, downward claw, new growth distortion, and edge cupping — direction and tissue age determine whether the cause is environmental, nutritional, or pest-related.

→ Leaf curl lesson
wilting

Wilting & Drooping

Both overwatering and underwatering cause drooping. Root damage and heat stress cause mid-day wilt. The texture of the drooping leaf and whether it recovers at lights-off separates these causes.

→ Water uptake lesson
damage

Pest Damage

Stippling, silvering, streaking, and new growth distortion all have specific pest causes that look different under magnification. A 60× loupe inspection is required before concluding pest cause.

→ Pest damage lesson
environment

Environmental Stress

Heat bleaching, cold purpling, wind damage, and VPD stress are distributed by canopy position — not leaf age. This distinguishes them from nutritional symptoms.

→ Environmental stress lesson

Symptom observation protocol

Describe the pattern

Colour change type, texture, shape. Neutral language — "interveinal yellowing on old leaves" not "magnesium deficiency."

Map distribution

Old or new tissue? Whole plant or one branch? Top, middle, or bottom of canopy? All plants or one?

Timeline

When did it start? Progressing or stable? What changed recently — feed, environment, training, IPM?

Root zone check

pH, EC, moisture, temperature. Most foliar symptoms originate here. Check before treating leaves.

Environment check

VPD, temperature, PPFD, airflow. Environmental symptoms are often misread as nutritional.

Pest inspection

60× loupe on leaf undersides, new growth, and stem bases. Rule out before concluding abiotic cause.

Build the differential

List all causes that fit the evidence. Test the most probable with the smallest intervention first.

Act and observe

Make one change. Wait 3–5 days. Evaluate whether the pattern improved, stabilised, or progressed.
