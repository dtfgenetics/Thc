# Part 10 Research Gaps

The public lessons should make these gaps visible instead of filling them with grower folklore.

1. **Standardized cannabis training trials are limited.** Many named techniques are widely used but have not been compared across enough cultivars, densities, environments, and repeated trials to support universal schedules.
2. **Technique names are not always standardized treatments.** Terms such as FIM, mainlining, lollipopping, and heavy defoliation can describe materially different interventions between growers and studies.
3. **Yield alone is an incomplete endpoint.** Architecture studies should also report spatial flower distribution, quality metrics, light uniformity, labor, recovery time, disease risk, and treatment intensity.
4. **Cultivar-by-treatment interactions need more attention.** A training method that benefits one architecture may impose unnecessary recovery cost on another.
5. **Canopy measurements are often underspecified.** Future comparisons should record where and when PPFD, canopy density, airflow, branch position, and leaf area were measured.
6. **Repeated-intervention cost is poorly characterized.** Multiple cuts, bends, ties, and defoliation events should be evaluated cumulatively rather than as isolated events.
7. **Mechanistic extrapolation needs labels.** Auxin biology, thigmomorphogenesis, compensatory photosynthesis, and source-sink concepts are useful, but general plant evidence should not be presented as direct cannabis treatment proof.

These gaps are not a reason to avoid training. They are a reason to document the intervention and evaluate the plant response instead of presenting a single grower convention as universal plant science.
