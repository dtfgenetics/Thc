# Training & Canopy Architecture

Cannabis training is best understood as **plant architecture management**, not as a list of tricks. Bending, pruning, trellising, shoot removal, and defoliation all change how leaves and branches occupy space, intercept light, exchange air, compete for assimilates, and recover from disturbance.

This topic asks four questions before any training action:

1. **What architectural problem are we trying to change?** Examples include excessive vertical dominance, crowded branch overlap, poor internal light, weak support, inaccessible canopy zones, or nonuniform branch distribution.
2. **What tissue is being changed or removed?** A flexible internode, shoot apex, axillary branch, lower sink, mature source leaf, or supported branch does not respond in the same way.
3. **How will we measure the response?** Useful records include canopy width and height, node and branch distribution, repeated PPFD measurements, airflow observations, recovery time, wound condition, and final crop measurements.
4. **What evidence actually supports the decision?** Direct cannabis trials are preferred when available. Broader plant physiology can explain mechanisms, but it should not be converted into universal cannabis schedules or guaranteed yield claims.

The practical goal is a canopy that can be **measured, observed, and compared**. A flatter or cleaner-looking plant is not automatically a better-performing plant. The useful result is the combination of architecture, light distribution, airflow, plant health, recovery, and crop outcome appropriate to the genotype and production system.
