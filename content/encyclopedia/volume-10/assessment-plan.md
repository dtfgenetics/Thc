# Part 10 Assessment Plan

The assessment layer should test reasoning rather than memorized grower schedules.

## Core competencies

A learner completing THC-ENC-181–190 should be able to:

1. distinguish genetic architecture from management-induced architecture;
2. explain why apical dominance, node development, tissue age, and mechanical condition affect training response;
3. separate bending, topping, partial meristem cuts, repeated structural training, trellising, lower-shoot pruning, and defoliation by the tissue and process each changes;
4. identify when a claim is supported by direct cannabis evidence versus broader plant physiology;
5. reject universal week, node-count, removal-percentage, and guaranteed-yield rules when the evidence does not support them;
6. define a measurable canopy objective before intervention;
7. design a before/after record using plant identity, stage, treatment detail, light measurements, architecture measurements, recovery, and final crop outcome;
8. recognize injury or poor recovery as a reason to stop or modify additional training.

## Knowledge-check style

Use scenario questions such as:

- Two cultivars under the same light develop different branch angles and internode lengths. Which observations are needed before attributing the difference to training?
- A grower removes many healthy fan leaves because lower sites are shaded. What measurements would determine whether the intervention helped?
- A trellised canopy looks flat but lower PPFD remains highly variable. What does that reveal about visual uniformity versus measured uniformity?
- A bent stem develops a crease and reduced growth above the bend. Which evidence distinguishes successful repositioning from mechanical injury?

Avoid trivia questions that reward memorizing a fixed training week or a universal number of nodes.
