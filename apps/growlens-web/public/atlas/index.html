How to use the Atlas

Structure → function → observation

A strong plant-science explanation connects what a structure is, what it does, what influences it, and what evidence would support or weaken a diagnosis.

01 · Structure

Identify the tissue or organ

Start with anatomy: age, position, tissue boundaries, vascular connections, reproductive structures, and the healthy baseline for that part of the plant.

Anatomy →
02 · Function

Connect it to physiology

Ask what the structure is doing: carbon fixation, transport, gas exchange, signaling, reproduction, defense, storage, support, or uptake.

Physiology →
03 · Evidence

Observe before assigning cause

Map location, distribution, time course, environment, root-zone data, pest evidence, recent changes, and recovery response before ranking explanations.

Developmental context

The same tissue means different things at different stages

Development changes source-sink relationships, architecture, reproductive priorities, tissue age, water demand, and the interpretation of visible symptoms.

1SeedEmbryo protected inside the seed coat.
2GerminationImbibition, radicle emergence, shoot establishment.
3SeedlingCotyledons transition toward true-leaf photosynthesis.
4Vegetative growthRapid leaf, root, stem, node, and branch expansion.
5Flower transitionMeristem identity and source-sink priorities change.
6Reproductive developmentFlowers, pollen, fertilization, seed, and maturation pathways.
⌕

Observation rule: a visible pattern is evidence, not proof of a single cause. Confirm location, progression, developmental stage, environment, root-zone conditions, pest/pathogen evidence, and recent changes before acting.
