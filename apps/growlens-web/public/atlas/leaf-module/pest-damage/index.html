Pest Damage on Leaves

Pest damage is identified by evidence, not guesswork. The leaf is inspected from the top, underside, petiole, vein junctions, and nearby new growth.

PreviousLeaf ModuleNext: Environmental Stress

Evidence to look for

  • Stippling, silvering, trails, holes, or edge chewing.
  • Eggs, larvae, mites, shed skins, frass, or webbing.
  • Distorted new growth or localized clusters of damage.
  • Movement on the underside or around vein junctions.

What to check

Use bright light and magnification. Inspect upper leaves, lower leaves, undersides, petioles, media surface, sticky traps, and neighboring plants before deciding on a response.

Common look-alikes

Nutrient spotting, foliar spray residue, physical abrasion, environmental speckling, disease lesions, and dust can be mistaken for pests without direct evidence.
